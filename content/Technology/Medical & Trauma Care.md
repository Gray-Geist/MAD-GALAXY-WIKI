# Medical & Trauma Care

*Called "Cut, Patch & Pray" by the grunts who live it.*

![[cyberware_medical_items_Medkit.png]]

---

## Overview

**Combat Trauma Response & Accelerated Recovery Doctrine (CTRARD)** is the cold, practical doctrine that keeps a body in the fight long enough to earn tomorrow's pain. It'll keep you fighting — but every patch, graft, and float leaves its own quiet scar. The flesh remembers what the mind tries to forget. You come back from the edge, but you never come back clean.

---

## Medical Materials & Systems

### Anaesthoskin
Stops the bleed and buys minutes. Applied to wounds to immediately seal and begin healing — clotting blood and preventing exsanguination. Comes in expanding bio-foam or nano skin forms. 100% biodegradable.

### Androx
A drug that relieves muscular strain, pain, and tension. Delivered by injection.

### Anes-7
Part three of a field interrogation kit. Megacorps promise "Complete unconsciousness in 7 seconds or less." This hypo-needle drug suppresses central nervous system activity, resulting in unconsciousness and total lack of sensation. The user wakes up extremely weakened.

### Bio-Printed Scaffolds *(aka "Meat Slabs" or "Franken-Guts")*
A portable bio-printer with a fat needle muzzle and a cryo-vial magazine. Feed it blood or a universal donor pack, punch in the wound profile — 90 seconds later it begins extruding warm, wet sheets of collagen, vessel, and half-grown organ tissue. Total printing time: 20–90 minutes depending on size.

In the field, an auto-doc or ripperdoc slices you open just enough to slap the slab onto the bleeding organ, then seals you with sutures or mesh. In a proper med-bay they'll crack the cavity wide and sew the patch in under full sterile light. Two to three days of sweating and shivering while it incorporates. A few weeks later — it's yours, scarred and ugly, but yours.

**Advantages:** Actually replaces the missing tissue with a near-perfect match; best short of a core-world clone vat; can be pre-printed in med-bays for planned ops.

**Limitations:** Requires significant power, space, sterile air, and time — rare outside dropships or med-bays. Universal donor grafts still fight you; without Immunoblockers you'll swell up like a corpse in atmo. Botched print and the patch liquefies inside you — septic shock within hours. Most users end up with a lumpy, tender ridge that aches when vessel pressure shifts. Costs a month's hazard pay — if the ripperdoc likes your face.

### Bounce Back / ReXGens
Medical inhaler or intravenous nanobots (brand name: **ReXGens**) that speed up healing by converting nearby resources plus internal reserves to quickly replace damaged cells — effectively fast healing. Powered by the body's thermal energy. Side effect: extreme hunger, as the body must compensate for lost heat during the healing process.

### Complidon / NX34
Part two of a field interrogation kit. Known as "Compliance in a syringe" — breaks down the mind and induces cognitive "malfunction." Destroys short-term memory for up to 48 hours before and after usage. Side effects: dangerously increased heart rate (often leading to cardiac arrest or stroke) and the feeling of being immersed in fire, causing extreme twitching. Counter-vaccines exist. **Banned substance in most star nations.**

### Florencium
A nanosteroid, weaker than 'Dorph, that reduces inflammation. Also reduces pain and makes the user alert and slightly more aggressive.

### Immunoblockers *(aka "Reject Shields" or "Cy-Sups")*
Immunosuppressive pharmaceuticals designed to dampen the body's rejection response to foreign implants — especially cybernetic augmentations. Developed by [[BioMega]] post-Savage Wars. Uses targeted nano-inhibitors that selectively bind to T-cell receptors, creating a "shield" around new implants while leaving the rest of the immune system functional. Standard dose via auto-injector or dermal patch lasts 48–72 hours.

**Overuse leads to "chrome rot"** — chronic immune fatigue where even minor wounds fester unchecked. Side effects: heightened vulnerability to viral agents, neural fog, and rare "rebound flares" where the immune system overcompensates and attacks the user's own tissues.

### Micro-Scaffolds *(aka "Flexi-graft" or "Itch-Weave")*
A ripperdoc or field medic applies a coin-sized applicator against the wound and the gun spits a lattice of smart-polymer threads laced with carbon nanotubes straight into the meat. It hurts like fire ants on a bender — the mesh is conductive, riding your own bioelectric field and knitting muscle and vessel along its grid. 6–12 hours later the threads start dissolving, replaced by your own scar tissue. 5–10 days and it's gone — if you're lucky.

**Advantages:** Fits in a belt pouch; works in vacuum or hard-burn; keeps you moving when you have no time to lie down and die.

**Limitations:** Feels like bugs burrowing the entire time; scratch too hard and you rip fresh clots. Overlap patches and you get "mesh burn" — hard ropey keloids that never quite soften. EMP or bad stun and the lattice can shred inside you, turning into slow sepsis. Edge price is whatever the doc's in debt for that week.

### Parallax
Part one of a field interrogation kit. Administered via hypo-needle. Immobilizes everything except sensory, cognitive, and speaking functions. **Banned substance in most star nations.**

### Psychatrex
A drug that nullifies empathic traces and telepathic influences. Developed to deal with Savage post-human tech discovered at Base 88.

### Regenerative Immersion Tanks *(aka "Regen Tanks" or "Slurry Coffins")*
A two-meter torpedo-shaped tube full of breathable perfluorocarbon slime kept at body temperature — tastes like burnt batteries and clings to your lungs like guilt. You're stripped, sedated, and slid in head-first. Gravity vanishes; every fracture and torn ligament sighs in relief while the slurry pumps oxygen and nanos straight into the wounds.

Clinically: a sealed fluid-filled cylinder of oxygenated perfluorocarbon emulsion mimicking body temperature. The patient is fully immersed and lightly sedated. Micro-pumps circulate nano-slurry past injuries — delivering dissolved oxygen, nutrients, stem-cell boosters, and targeted nanoswarms to damaged sites. Buoyancy removes all weight-bearing stress.

**Standard cycle:** 6–48 hours for serious to severe trauma; up to 5 days for critical.

**Advantages:** Heals 4–8 times faster than a cot. Broken spines set straight. Crushed pelvises knit without pins. Critical trauma heals. The only reason some troopers ever walk again.

**Limitations:** Patient is blind, deaf, and dreaming the entire time. Stay past 72 hours and **"float psychosis"** sets in — hallucinations of drowning in vacuum, voices telling you to open the hatch and breathe the black. Those in too long are called **"Floaters"** or **"Drowners."** The slurry leaves a chemical taste in your throat for weeks. Half the veterans who've spent serious time in one flinch at the smell of lothranium. Tanks are stationary and power-hungry — lose the generator and you wake up boiling alive or frozen solid.

### Smart-Tourniquet
A semi-nano tourniquet that when applied to a wound, administers medically appropriate pressure and adapts to an ambulatory patient and changing vitals.

### 'Dorph (Synthetic Endorphin)
See [[Food, Drink & Substances]]. Military-grade painkiller. Reduces pain, stress, and fatigue; produces a "second wind" rush. Risk of permanent nerve damage — permanent loss of coordination, reflexes, and dexterity.

## Aim Assist Drug (AADs) — "Carrots"
Originally used in professional sports to greatly increase hand-eye coordination. After it wears off, vision becomes blurred and hands shake.

## CTRARD Protocols — Step by Step

> *What happens to you from the moment you take a hit, to the moment you walk again — or don't.*

### 1 — Immediate Bleeding Control *(0–5 minutes)*
**Anaesthoskin** foam or **Smart-Tourniquets** buy the first minutes. Foam hisses out, expands, and clamps arteries shut. Tourniquets auto-cinch to vitals, then ease just enough when you run so the limb doesn't rot off before exfil.
*Reality check: you're still leaking — just slower.*

### 2 — Field Stabilization *(5–60 minutes)*
**Bounce Back inhaler or IV ReXGens** hit the bloodstream. The nanoswarm burns body heat like a furnace and starts knitting tissue with whatever calories are floating around. You shiver while the holes close. Eat — or pass out.

### 3 — Soft-Tissue Gunshots & Shrapnel *(Hours to Days)*
**Micro-scaffolds ("Itch-Weave")** go in next. The applicator punches the mesh lattice straight through the wound channel — fire ants and razor wire under the skin for six hours while it guides regrowth along the bioelectric field. Five to ten days of itching, then it dissolves. If the wound is bigger than a fist, you're waiting for a med-bay.

### 4 — Moderate to Severe Organ & Tissue Loss *(1–21 Days)*
**Bio-printed scaffolds** replace what's now floating in vacuum. Field auto-doc cracks you open just enough to slap the warm wet slab onto the bleeding organ. Two days of fever while it vascularizes, three weeks until the ridge aches when station pressure shifts. Botched print — the patch liquefies — septic shock in six hours.

### 5 — Severe to Critical Polytrauma *(6 Hours–14 Days)*
**Regen tank ("Slurry Coffin")** is the nuclear option for when the body has been folded, spindled, and mutilated — multiple organ hits, crushed pelvises, spinal trauma. They slide you into the **Black Womb**: zero-load perfluorocarbon that lets bones set straight and ligaments sigh in relief. Six to forty-eight hours for most; five days if you're held together by spite.

Simple long-bone fractures rarely rate a tank — splints and mesh do the job — but when the skeleton's a bag of gravel or the spine's compromised, the tank is the only reason some troopers walk again. Come out tasting coolant for weeks and flinching at the smell of lothranium. Stay too long and float psychosis sets in — troopers have tried to open the hatch and breathe vacuum because the voices told them to.

### 6 — Cyber Integration & Rejection Management
New chrome going in hot? **Immunoblockers ("Cy-Sups")** for 48–72 hours while the body decides if it's going to murder the implant, live with it, or just kill you. Overuse and you get chrome rot — wounds fester, station flu turns lethal, and one day a paper cut kills you. That's the price.
