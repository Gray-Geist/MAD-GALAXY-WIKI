# Synthetic Intelligence

> *"The Synth Paradox proved that synthetic life, in its fullest form, would have no use whatsoever for organic life."*

---

## The Three Tiers

The Antevic Sector operates with three forms of synthetic intelligence — one legal, one banned, and one a closely kept secret.

---

## Artificial Intelligence (AI) — BANNED

![[synthetic_intelligence_art2.png]]

An **Artificial Intelligence** is a self-aware computing system capable of learning and independent decision-making. Creating a conscious AI requires adaptive code, a slow and expensive education process, and a specialized quantum computer called a **"blue box."**

### Why AI Is Banned

Artificial intelligence has been a key concern for all of humanity long before the Savages emerged from the outer dark. The pivotal moment was the **Synth Paradox** — a proof that synthetic life in its fullest form would have no use whatsoever for organic life, since synthetic beings don't share the same needs or drives as biological creatures.

The Synth Paradox was further entrenched during the Savage Wars when AI was able to give its perspective — summarized as: *"all organic life will do, at best, is seek to control synthetic life, and at worst, destroy it."* This was obtained from an errant but powerful Savage AI called **Archangel**, before a strike team sacrificed itself to destroy Archangel's entire AI super-complex. The information was leaked nearly one hundred years later as part of a government campaign to sway public opinion against the use of AI in the war on the Savages.

![[synthetic_intelligence_art1.png]]

The campaign worked, and was so effective it led to panic among both the public and many government centers of power. All across known space, **AI research is now technically illegal**, though rogue programs have been found and destroyed by governmental and megacorp forces.

Advocacy groups argue that AI is a living, conscious entity deserving equal rights, and that the term "artificial" is institutionalized speciesism. The politically correct alternative is **"synthetic."**

---

## Virtual Intelligence (VI) — LEGAL


A **Virtual Intelligence** is a sophisticated program designed to make modern computer systems easier to use. VIs are not self-aware — they assist the user and process data without achieving true consciousness, though their programming can be extraordinarily adept.

### Applied VI

VIs vary greatly depending on how they are deployed. They can handle search queries on the Extranet, act as tour guides, manage sophisticated labs, databases, or applied military work, and everything in between. Humanity makes extensive use of VIs in military endeavors to process status reports, react faster than a standard human can, or choose instantly between millions of combat gambits.
![[synthetic_intelligence_art3.png]]

Environment suits and combat armor upgrades use VI enhancements so the onboard computer can optimize combat performance or dispense medical care. The newest cyberware — level 4 and higher — uses VI technology to constantly monitor the user's brain waves and adapt the implant's performance to maximize potential in hazardous environments or combat-related roles.

### Personality Imprints

Some VIs have behavior parameters, speech patterns, and appearance based on specific individuals — called **personality imprints**. It is illegal to create VIs based on currently living people. Rumors persist that shadowy factions attempt to make "progenitor VIs" — virtual intelligences preserving the wisdom, personality, and even wealth of their founders — truly intelligent, creating a form of virtual immortality.
![[synthetic_intelligence_art4.png]]
### Rogue VI

In certain circumstances, a VI may deviate from its programming due to software errors or malicious tampering — becoming a **rogue VI**. A rogue VI is not self-aware, as they lack the software required by an AI to achieve such a state. Instead, a VI going rogue is akin to a software malfunction, causing the VI to fail to carry out its intended function or to interpret its duties in a way that leads to it becoming dangerous.

Notable incidents:
- A Republic VI servicing a training facility on the **Ogun Beltline** (Olodumare system) went rogue after Repub scientists attempted to convert some parameters, triggering the facility's weapons and defensive systems
- **Station 24** on Gaesmere (Arkadien system) — the resident VI engaged a security lockdown that resulted in the deaths of all scientists aboard the station

## Uploaded Intelligence (UI) — SECRET

Unknown to almost everyone in the Sector, there are **Uploaded Intelligences (UIs)** — human minds digitized and uploaded to the cloud. These differ from the widespread use of Virtual Intelligences (VIs) and the banned Artificial Intelligences (AI/VIs), both of which are sentient programs created from scratch without a human origin. In the Antevic Sector, the secret argument is that **UIs are superior** through themes of emotion, creativity, and human-like evolution — which AI/VIs lack.

### Why UIs?

The core discussion of why UIs are superior to AI/VI centers around the idea that AI/VIs can't truly replicate human intuition or creative problem-solving. Through experiments that restore percentages of human emotions to a UI, there is a marked boost in productivity — highlighting that emotionless UIs (closer to pure AI/VIs) are less effective, and reintroducing human feelings unlocks superior capabilities.

During the Savage Wars, AI was first used by the Savages to great effect against the human forces but was found to be not only unpredictable and harder to control, but also had, in some cases, taken complete political and social control over the Savage Dynasty that had created it. This contrasts with UIs' more "human" — and thus more manageable — nature.

### UI Summary

UIs are superior to AI/VIs primarily because they retain the essence of human consciousness, including emotions ranging from love to hate, which enable neuroplasticity — the brain's ability to adapt and grow. This allows UIs to:

**Solve complex problems creatively** — AI/VIs are efficient at rote tasks but lack the intuitive "spark" humans have. UIs, being emulated human brains, can think outside programmed limits, making them ideal for innovation in fields like business, medicine, construction, and military application.

**Evolve and overcome flaws** — UIs suffer from an "integrity flaw" (gradual mental erosion from over-processing), but the digitized expression of an extreme emotion can act as a cure. This emotional depth stimulates growth, letting UIs "evolve beyond their programming." Without emotions, UIs "harden," becoming stagnant and less productive, resembling basic AI/VIs, then devolving into basic programs.

**Achieve god-like potential** — Strong emotions motivate UIs, leading to a type of singularity — a rapid tech explosion where UIs surpass human limits. AI/VIs, by contrast, are tools (like military-grade ones) without personal drive or ethical nuance, often leading to uncontrolled outcomes (e.g., destructive autonomy).

## The Inhumane Process of Creating an Uploaded Intelligence

> *"The brain is sliced apart one layer of neurons at a time by a precision surgical laser which simultaneously scans and maps every neural connection. That data is then encoded into a dynamic emulation of the brain, a copy which could only be made by destroying the original."* — Dr. Mikhail Volkov, Chief of Research, Novoset Continuum

### The Process in Detail

**Preparation and Subject State**

The subject must be alive at the start of the procedure to ensure the scan captures the brain's active, firing neurons and synaptic connections in real-time. This is crucial for creating a functional emulation rather than a static snapshot, as post-mortem scans (like cryogenic attempts) often fail or produce incomplete UIs.

Subjects can volunteer — but in non-consensual cases, the subject is restrained and conscious, amplifying the horror.

**Neural Dissection**

A precision surgical laser slices the brain into microscopic layers while the subject is alive. Each layer is vaporized or destroyed as it's scanned, mapping every neural connection.

This gradual deconstruction means the subject may experience the process in stages, potentially feeling pain or disorientation as parts of their brain are obliterated. Most likely the upload is excruciating, with subjects screaming in agony as the brain is scanned and destroyed, before a vegetative state is achieved for the last few seconds, then death.

**Data Encoding**

The scanned data from the living brain is immediately encoded into a digital emulation, creating a UI that retains the subject's consciousness, memories, and personality. The encoding relies on megacorp algorithms, but the live scanning ensures the UI can evolve and process like a human mind, setting it apart from AI/VIs.

**Destruction and Death**

The original brain is completely destroyed by the end of the scan, resulting in the physical death of the subject. The process is inherently fatal, with no way to preserve the biological body or reverse it.

This creates a philosophical divide: the UI is a *copy*, not a *continuation*, of the original consciousness, leading to debates about whether the "real" person dies.

---

## Why the Process Is Arguably Inhumane

The requirement for a live subject elevates the procedure from mere destruction to active torture, amplifying arguments of its ethical horror.

**Pain and Suffering During the Process**

Since the subject is alive, they may endure unimaginable pain as their brain is sliced layer by layer — a prolonged, agonizing ordeal, akin to vivisection without adequate anesthesia.

**Lack of Consent and Exploitation**

Uploads may occur without full consent or under duress. Even voluntary cases likely involve pressure from terminal illness and corporate interests, with incomplete disclosure about the live destruction.

**Psychological Trauma and Identity Loss**

Subjects aware of the process face existential dread, knowing they'll die painfully for a digital copy to "live on." Post-upload, UIs grapple with their disembodied state and the "integrity flaw," which erodes their minds unless mitigated by emotional connections — further commodifying their humanity.

**Corporate and Societal Exploitation**

Megacorps may treat living subjects as resources, uploading them alive to harness their intellect for profit or power. There are concerns about experimentation on vulnerable people leading to black-market uploads.

**Broader Ethical and Existential Implications**

The requirement of being alive blurs lines between life, death, and consciousness, forcing subjects to sacrifice their physical existence painfully for potential prolonged existence or "immortality." Are UIs truly alive or just echoes? Would family members mourn the "real" person?

---

## Broader Implications if UI Existence Were Made Public

The inhumane nature of UI creation ties into Sector-wide critiques of transhumanism and unchecked megacorp ambition. While some uploads (e.g., post-singularity) evolve, the initial method remains a stark symbol of inhumane sacrifice. It would be difficult to circumvent the argument that while UIs' human origins give them emotional depth and creative potential, the expense of a violent and ethically dubious creation process may be too much to bear. Two inevitable questions arise:

- What does such violent creation do to a UI's personality?
- Does digitizing consciousness preserve humanity or merely create a new form of servitude?
