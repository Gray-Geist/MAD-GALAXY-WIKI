# Cosmic Strands

> *"Strands are turbulent forces 'stitched into' the galaxy."*

---

## What Are Cosmic Strands?

**Cosmic Strands** are strands of energy believed to have formed from the inferno of forces at the beginning of the universe. They are chained between gravity wells — stars, planets, and other centers of mass — creating natural corridors through spacetime that [[Skip Drives & FTL Travel|skip drives]] exploit for faster-than-light travel.

On starmaps, Strands are represented as **lines between star systems** — abstractly linear corridors of spacetime where skip drives work more efficiently. **Super Strands** have a greater effect; **Weak Strands** have a lesser effect.

---

## Physics of the Strands

Strands leverage **gravity** to warp spacetime into navigable corridors, using the natural curvature caused by massive celestial bodies as anchors. **Electromagnetism** plays an equally critical role, stabilizing the exotic particles emitted along the Strands — these particles create a navigable "sheath" around each Strand, protecting ships from the chaotic effects of raw spacetime distortion and enabling precise FTL traversal.

This interplay between gravity and electromagnetism explains why certain stars or planetary systems can **enhance or destabilize** Strand behavior, introducing turbulence, cycles, and obstacle ratios.

### Gravity Wells and Strands
Studies have shown that gravity wells don't *create* Strands but can *manipulate* their properties — pulling weaker Strands into existence or amplifying existing ones.

### Rogue Strands
**Rogue Strands** occur spontaneously, appearing to go nowhere. They are hypothesized to lead to destinations impossibly distant or currently beyond comprehension. In some cases, a weak Strand may connect to a rogue planet — occasionally useful for those who want a one-way trip to an isolated colony.

## Strand Types

| Type | Efficiency | Travel Time (per system) |
|---|---|---|
| Super Strand | Highest | ~1 week |
| Stable Strand | Standard | ~1.5 weeks |

*Note: No formula has yet been found to explain the exact relationship, as Strand turbulence, cycles, and obstacle ratios affect FTL travel unpredictably.*

## A Catastrophe

The ship's black box tells a grim story. Pursued by a privateer fleet, a corsair captain made the fateful decision to jump near the gravity well of a blue giant star. The ship's VI issued its automated warning. The crew pounded on the command deck door, screaming to stop. The captain's response was curt: *"Do it."*

The drive engaged. Gravimetric shear surged. The vessel was violently expelled from the Strand like a shotgun blast — fragmented across a vast expanse for millions of kilometers. Some fragments bore scorch marks of intense frictional heat. Others fused into grotesque crystalline structures from spatial compression. The ship's spine was found embedded in a rogue planet's surface.

No bodies were recovered.

The captain's final whisper in the black box: *"Better this than their chains."*

This jump failure was recorded and released to the public, garnering billions of views. A second famous incident — the **"spaghettification" video** — shows a ship stretching into impossibly long fragments near a planetary gravity well before spiraling into oblivion.

Spacers are superstitious folk. Prayers, wards, and rituals before a skip are common.
