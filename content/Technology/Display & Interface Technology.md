# Display & Interface Technology

*"The difference between a room and a window is information."*

---

## Overview

Display and interface technology in the Sector has long since outpaced the physical hardware it runs on. Screens as discrete objects — frames holding flat panels at fixed points in a room — were a transitional phase. What replaced them was a design philosophy built around the assumption that any surface can be an interface, and any environment can be reconfigured by light.

---

## Terminal Devices

The cortical interface handles calls, messages, news, identity verification, and the everyday data transactions of modern urban life — but it lacks the processing headroom and specialized interfaces required for high-stakes military operations and demanding professional data streams. Terminal devices act as external extensions to the thread, enabling advanced slicing, precision control, and secure remote access where the cortical interface alone cannot reach. They bridge the gap between personal neural communications and the hard requirements of military, scientific, and corporate operations across the Sector.

Three tiers of terminal device are in common circulation:

### Battleboards

![[tech_battle_board.png]]

Designed for military-grade slicing, real-time battlefield command, and electronic warfare operations. Battleboards provide processing power well beyond what the cortical interface can sustain — running parallel encryption stacks, sensor overlays, and active intrusion suites simultaneously without thermal throttling.

Physically, they are built to survive the field: ultra-fast processors behind reinforced shock-absorbing housings, flexible durable displays that take impacts without shattering, foldable profiles for pack storage. A Battleboard on a slicer's knee in a contested zone looks nothing like consumer hardware — and isn't meant to.

*Notable models:* The **ViperEdge X-7** (Tri-Tachyon) is optimized for slicers, featuring advanced encryption and real-time sensor overlays. The **Stratos MK-II** (Gogol Combine) emphasizes rapid data processing and stealth-mode capability for covert operations.

### Terminal Boards

![[tech_terminal_board.jpg]]

Tailored for specialized professional fields — science, manufacturing, construction, corporate operations. Terminal Boards offer industry-specific applications, data visualization, and control functions that go well beyond everyday communication. Where a Battleboard is built for hostile environments and adversarial networks, a Terminal Board is built for depth: the modular interfaces, customizable software, and precision toolsets that professional workflows demand.

High-resolution displays with precision touch, gesture recognition, and augmented reality overlays are standard. A structural engineer walking a construction site and a xenobiologist analyzing field samples may carry the same hardware class — configured entirely differently.

*Notable models:* The **Nexus 22** (Tri-Tachyon) features advanced simulation software for scientific research and manufacturing diagnostics. The **ChronosPro** (Gogol Combine) is optimized for construction and urban planning with integrated 3D modeling tools.

### Hand Terminals

![[tech_hand_terminal.png]]

Compact, multifunctional, and carried by nearly everyone who needs more than a cortical interface alone provides. The Hand Terminal serves as a personal data manager, remote control interface, and access key for secure networks — a physical anchor point for identity and authorization in environments that demand something tangible.

The defining aesthetic is the display: a sleek, solid rectangular screen of glass silicate or transparent polycarbonate, visually and tactilely distinct from flexible consumer slabs. A robust cube-shaped housing at the base contains the circuitry and physical components. Interaction supports touch, proximal motion, gesture detection, and holographic projection through nearby emitter devices.

*Notable models:* The **ClearComm H-1** (Tri-Tachyon) is known for seamless cortical interface integration and high-security protocols. The **VisionCube XR** (Gogol Combine) excels in remote operations and real-time communications, functioning as a portable command node in the field.

## HardLight Windows

HardLight Window technology emerged as a practical evolution in secure visibility and communication — born from humanity's need for something more versatile than reinforced glass or holographic displays. Glass shatters. Holograms lack physical substance. The HardLight Window combines a solid, durable barrier with photoluminescent projection technology, allowing high-definition real-time display on its surface while maintaining the integrity of a physical divide.

Its development was driven by the harsh realities of life beyond Old Earth, where conventional solutions failed under pressure differentials, dangerous environmental anomalies, and the need for airtight quarantine. A viewport that can crack under hull stress is a liability. A holographic display that a prisoner can simply walk through is no barrier at all. HardLight addressed both problems simultaneously.

The technology found ubiquitous application across transportation — vehicles, vessels, starship brigs — as well as prisons, research facilities, and any environment requiring both observation and isolation. It allows supervisors to monitor and communicate with high-risk individuals or volatile environments without the vulnerabilities of conventional surveillance systems, which can be hacked, obscured, or physically destroyed. In laboratories, it lets scientists oversee dangerous experiments from behind a barrier that is simultaneously a window, a display surface, and a quarantine seal.

Unlike camera feeds, HardLight provides intuitive face-to-face interaction — critical for interrogations, delicate negotiations, or any situation where the human dimension of observation matters. Its ability to toggle visibility in both directions gives it a psychological dimension that cameras cannot replicate: the window can be a connection or a one-way mirror, offering controlled anonymity or deliberate presence as the situation demands.

The distinction between HardLight and conventional holographic projection is tactile as much as visual. Standard holographics are transparent to touch — a hand passes through them. HardLight constructs resist light pressure; a palm pressed against the surface meets faint resistance, enough to feel like glass. Higher-density military and intelligence installations use emitter arrays capable of generating constructs solid enough to function as temporary barriers, obscure thermal signatures, or create false interior geometries that mislead surveillance optics — applications restricted under most faction law.

HardLight requires consistent power and a clean emitter array. Damaged or underpowered arrays produce characteristic artifacts — flickering edges, color drift, partial transparency — immediately recognizable to anyone who has spent time around the technology. In environments where the power grid is unreliable, HardLight Windows are the first luxury to fail.
