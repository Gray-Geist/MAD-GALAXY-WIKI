# Armor & Apparel

> [!info] Availability Codes
> All armor entries carry an **AV (Availability) code** indicating how difficult the item is to obtain. For a full explanation of all codes and acquisition mechanics, see [[Availability Codes]].

*Six centuries of warfare have produced a protective equipment ecosystem ranging from laminate law enforcement vests to fully sealed military battle systems and powered exoskeleton chassis. Below is the current state of the art.*

---

## Personal Armor

Personal armor in the Antevic Sector runs from the cheapest laminate vest a law enforcement budget can afford to purpose-built vacuum-rated contractor rigs — there is armor you buy and armor you are issued — the difference is not just money, but what someone has decided your life is worth.

---

### CRV-4 Composite Vest

![[CRV-4 Composite Vest.png]]

Passes for body armor when the organization issuing it doesn't expect a real fight. Lightweight laminate construction over a rigid ceramic insert, no environmental sealing, no integrated monitoring — just enough protection to stop a handgun round and satisfy an insurance liability clause. Standard issue for planetary law enforcement, private security details, and anyone whose employer calls their job "low-risk." On a battlefield it is nearly invisible, which is appropriate, because the people wearing it generally are too.

---

### SCA-12 Standard Combat Armor

![[SCA-12 Standard Combat Armor.png]]

Garrison and militia armor — the gear issued to soldiers who are expected to hold a line rather than break one. This is what you hand a soldier when you need them equipped fast and you're not sure how bad it will get. It has the vitals monitoring, which means the medic gets your biometrics the moment something goes wrong — that one feature has saved more lives than the armor plates themselves. It shows up in surplus, on militia rosters, in the armories of colonial governments that can't afford better and know it.

---

### MCA-7 Medium Combat Armor

![[MCA-7 Medium Combat Armor.png]]

Combat armor without the full military footprint. The MCA-7 is where the private sector intersects with real violence — medium-grade composites durable enough for sustained combat, light enough to function in it, and clean enough to be purchased without explaining too much to anyone. Plus vitals monitoring, optional TacLink integration, but no vacuum seal. Half the mercenary companies in the Sector run MCA-7 as their standard kit. The other half wish they did.

---

### MCA-6V "Vac-Rated"

![[MCA-6V Vac-Rated.png|600]]

![[MCA-6V Vac-Rated2.png|653]]

There are places in the Sector where the air itself is the threat, and for those places the MCA-6V exists. Preferred by salvage crews, void contractors, and mercenaries who operate across multiple environments without wanting to swap kits between them. The vacuum rating adds cost and a little weight but removes the need to think about what the atmosphere is doing to you when other things are also trying to.

---

## Battle Armor

Battle armor emerged from the Savage Wars as the minimum viable kit for a soldier expected to survive sustained contact — not just stop a round, but operate sealed against hostile atmospheres, maintain a TacLink feed, and still be trackable by a medic when things went wrong. Each star nation has or outsources its own BCL standard fitted with a closed architecture keyed to their own encrypted network. The BCL line represents that doctrine codified: a hierarchy of protection, weight, and capability built around the assumption that the environment itself is as dangerous as the enemy. These are not suits you wear to feel safer. They are systems built around the premise that you are going somewhere the galaxy is actively trying to kill you.

---

### BCL-3 Battle Armor

![[BCL-3 Battle Armor.png|697]]

![[BCL-3 Battle Armor2.png|697]]

The front-line military dress and the baseline against which every other armor in this tier is measured. Full environmental seal, integrated TacLink, vitals monitoring, air supply — everything a combat soldier needs to function in a denied environment and be tracked and treated when they go down. It is heavier than anything below it, and you feel it over the course of a long patrol. Surplus flows out of the military pipeline and into contractor hands at a steady rate. A used BCL-3 bought from a cagey dealer is still BCL-3.

---

### BCL-3D "Drop Variant"

The armor of the drop insertion specialist, also known as "Helljumpers" — this is an advanced BCL-3 chassis with extended air supply and pod interface hardpoints built directly into the frame. It does not announce what it is on the outside. What it carries is logistics: the extended air supply for long operational holds in denied environments, and a telemetry handshake with the drop pod that feeds the wearer a pre-landing picture of the insertion zone before they hit the ground. Who wears it is a narrower population than who wears BCL-3. These are units that arrive without a ship beneath them, in places that aren't expecting them, and need the armor to support that specific kind of work. Outside those units, a BCL-3D in the wild means asking pointed questions about how it got there.

---

## Assisted Combat Personnel Armor (ACPA)

![[armor_acpa.png|697]]

ACPA, or power armor — evolved out of the Savage Wars as the only reliable response to opponents in heavy carapace that standard arms couldn't break — a powered exoskeleton built around the principle that if you can't outrun the scale of violence the Savages brought, you absorb it instead. The first time you step into an ACPA chassis and feel the servos come online, you understand immediately why infantry without them are a different category of soldier. The chassis — the linear frame at the heart of every ACPA unit — does not just carry the suit's weight — it multiplies everything you do, and it does it quietly, translating servo power into an extension of battlefield power. Variants define their role by what the chassis prioritizes: protection, speed, stealth, firepower. Operating ACPA is not simply wearing better armor. It is becoming a different kind of problem on a battlefield — one that requires a different kind of solution to remove. Each star nation has or outsources its own ACPA standard fitted with a closed architecture keyed to their own encrypted network.

---

### ACPA-R "Republic Standard"

The jack of all trades — this is the chassis everything else is compared against — not because it is the best at any one thing, but because it is adequate at all of them. General purpose, general issue, unremarkable yet it makes the user remarkable in the best possible ways. The soldiers who wear it don't want a specialty chassis. They want armor that works, and the R delivers that without ceremony. It is the most common ACPA in the Sector by volume, which means it is also the most common powered armor a hostile force will ever have to solve for.

---

### ACPA-91 "Jackrabbit"

Speed before armor, always. The Jackrabbit is the ACPA for operators who trust their ability to not be where the fire is more than they trust their plates to stop it. The lateral thrust system — low-profile vectoring jets recessed into the hip and shoulder chassis — can create rapid sidestep movement with a control signature so clean that the motion reads almost like instinct from inside the suit. Recon units love it. So do operators who need to close a distance faster than an opponent can track. The chassis is not built to take sustained hits — it is built to not receive one.

---

### ACPA-B "Bulwark"

The Bulwark exists to be shot at. The integrated shield system is not a hand-held accessory — it is wired directly into the chassis' front arc — making the Bulwark's forward profile the hardest thing on a standard breach team to neutralize. Designed for the point-man role: first through the door, first into the corridor, the chassis that absorbs the initial response so the rest of the team can work. The additional frontal protection comes with the same mobility profile as the R standard — no sprint restriction, no speed penalty — which means the Bulwark can push and push fast. Operators who run it describe the experience of stacking at a breach point in full Bulwark as one of the few moments in combat where you are quietly certain you are the most dangerous thing in the immediate vicinity.

---

### ACPA-86 "Swinery"

This is a siege engine that has agreed to carry a soldier inside it. Immune to infantry explosives, frag, and small arms — it is incapable of sprinting. It does not pursue or flank. It stands in a place and makes that place extremely expensive to contest. Operators know this going in. The job of the Swinery is not to maneuver — it is to be immovable. In the right place at the right time, nothing in a standard infantry engagement can displace it. Finding one outside a siege detachment or MRRII black market channel means someone paid an extraordinary amount for a very specific kind of problem.

---

## Protective Suits

Environmental and hazard protection — from vacuum compression suits to CBRN-rated HAZMAT systems. These are not armor in the ballistic sense; they are armor against the environment. The common thread is the seal: everything in this section lives or dies on its ability to keep what's outside from getting in. See [[#Suit Breach]] for rules on what happens when that seal fails.

---

### Full Face Rebreather Mask

AV: C. Full-face respirator with integrated filter cartridges. Blocks airborne chemical and biological contaminants — gas, biotoxic clouds, atmospheric particulates. Standard emergency kit across any environment where the air quality isn't guaranteed. Filter cartridges degrade over time in contaminated conditions. Not sealed against vacuum — this is a filtration device, not an environmental suit.

---

### BHZ-1 Biohazard Suit

AV: R. Sealed bodysuit for chemical and biological hazard environments. Built-in comm unit. The standard choice for hazmat response teams, medical personnel in outbreak conditions, and anyone who needs a sealed barrier without the bulk of military-grade equipment. Radiation attenuation while sealed. Seal integrity is everything — a single breach ends the protection immediately, and improvised repair cannot restore contamination protection.

---

### HZX-3 HAZMAT Suit

AV: RM. Military-specification HAZMAT suit with reinforced outer shell and extended air supply over the civilian BHZ-1. Issued to CBRN specialist units across all Star Nation armed forces; leaks into military surplus channels at a steady rate. Bulkier than the BHZ-1 — operators move slower in it. Same vulnerability: a breach ends contamination protection immediately, and improvised repair will not restore it.

---

### CSX-4 Civilian Compression Suit

AV: RR. Light compression suit for vacuum and hostile atmosphere operation. The workhorse of the civilian void-worker market — salvagers, frontier colonists, maintenance crews working pressurized corridors with a history of seal failures. Full environmental seal, HUD, helmet comm. Not a combat suit — AR 0, vacuum protection only. The viewport restricts peripheral vision. Any hit at all is a breach.

---

### CSX-7 Military Compression Suit

AV: MRR. Combat-rated compression suit for void operations. The same vacuum protection as the CSX-4 with enough ballistic hardening to survive a firefight — barely. Preferred by void infantry and boarding specialists who need to close with an enemy in a vacuum environment and stay functional afterward. Optional TacLink integration.

---

### EVX-9 Void Hardsuit

AV: RR. Heavy EVA hardsuit for sustained zero-G and hostile environment operations. Fully articulated joints, integrated thruster pack, HUD, comm array, and tether hard points. Built for EVA specialists and deep-environment salvagers who need to operate tether-free for extended periods. The thruster pack provides meaningful maneuvering authority in zero-G — the difference between drifting off a hull and staying on task. Significant mobility penalty in standard gravity.

---

## Helmets, Shields & Soft Protective Gear

---

### TAC-H Combat Helmet

AV: MC. Tactical helmet with integrated IR sight, helmet cam, and IFF transponder. Sold separately for operators running MCA-7 or lighter armor who need TacLink connectivity without BCL-3. The IFF transponder keeps automated sentry systems from painting the wearer as a target regardless of what armor they're wearing. Ubiquitous in contractor and mercenary loadouts.

---

### BCS-3 Ballistic Combat Shield

AV: R. Lightweight plastisteel shield for breach scenarios and close-protection operations. Readied as a deliberate action — this is a tool of controlled engagements, not reactive firefights. Effective against small arms. Heavier weapons — LMGs, slaggers, launchers — defeat it on the first hit. Standard issue for law enforcement breach teams and executive protection details.

---

### Life Vest

AV: C. Keeps an unconscious or incapacitated wearer face-up and afloat without effort. Built-in hi-beam flashlight and solar beacon that broadcasts PDT data within a 2 km radius. Standard kit for dropship crews, pilots, and any operator deploying near open water.

---

## Clothing & Apparel

---

### BDU Field Uniform

AV: C. Durable field uniform in environment-appropriate camouflage pattern. Standard issue across all Star Nation ground forces. Available in Woodland, Desert, Arctic, Void (matte black), Urban, and Jungle patterns. Widely available through surplus channels — the baseline field garment of the Sector.

---

### Pilot G-Suit

AV: R. Full-body pressure suit worn by pilots and vehicle crew to counteract g-force during high-acceleration maneuvers — specifically when gravity dampeners are unavailable or overwhelmed. Standard issue for MOS 10 pilots and all starfighter crew. Not compatible with heavy armor.

---

### Cold Weather Gear

AV: C. Insulated field jacket rated for extreme cold environments. Packable. The item that separates a mission on an ice world from a medical evacuation.

---

### Expedition Fatigues

AV: C. Thermal-regulating undersuit for extended fieldwork in variable climates. Worn under armor — compatible with all standard armor types. The baseline protection against the attrition of operating in environments the body wasn't designed for.

---

### Camouflage Suit

![[Camouflage Suit.png]]

AV: R. Environment-specific camouflage suit for precision concealment. The choice of snipers, forward observers, and recon specialists who need to stay in one place for a long time without being found. No electronic components — immune to sensor sweeps, immune to jamming. Must be calibrated to the specific environment; the wrong pattern is no better than standard fatigues. Incompatible with BCL-3 and above from bulk.

---

### Aquatic Gear Set

AV: C. Propulsion fins and inflatable flotation vest. The fin set accelerates underwater movement significantly; the life vest handles the survival problem if the mission goes into water unexpectedly. Sold as a set. Standard kit for maritime operations and any deployment near significant bodies of water.

---

### Specialty Footwear

AV: C. Specialist footwear for specific operating environments. Three variants: Standard Field Boots with magnetic-sole option for zero-G deck operations; Jungle/Wetland Boots with waterproof construction; Thermal Boots with extreme-cold insulation. The right footwear stops being a comfort consideration and starts being an operational one the moment the terrain turns against the mission.

---

### Ultra Diffusive Environmental Poncho

![[Ultra Diffusive Environmental Poncho.png]]

AV: R. Treated camouflage rain cover with infrared-absorbing coating. The practical concealment choice for operators who need something that works on the move and over any armor — including BCL-3 and ACPA, where nothing else fits. Less effective for dedicated concealment than the Camouflage Suit, but it doesn't require recalibration, doesn't restrict armor choice, and works whether the operator is stationary or moving. The camo cape of the Sector's infantry — general-purpose, versatile, and always in the kit.

## Suit Breach

All sealed armor — everything with an environmental seal and an Air Supply rating — shares a common vulnerability: a hit that punches through the armor and reaches the wearer also compromises the seal.

**Trigger:** When a hit deals damage that gets through armor (any damage after AR reduction reaches Health), the suit is breached. Reduce Air Supply by 1 immediately.

**While breached, the suit is leaking.** Air Supply rolls cost **2 rating points per ☠** instead of the normal 1, and each subsequent hit that deals damage through armor reduces Air Supply by 1 again — the breach widens under continued stress.

Note for AR 0 suits (CSX-4 and similar): any hit at all causes a breach, since there is no armor to absorb it.

**Sealing the breach — two options:**

- **Armor Sealant Kit (FieldSeal Patch):** Quick Action. Seal fully restored — Air Supply rolls return to normal (1 rating point per ☠), and additional hits no longer trigger passive Air Supply loss. The right tool, the right answer.

- **Field Improvisation (no kit):** Full Action. HEAVY MACHINERY roll. On success: breach stabilized — additional hits no longer trigger passive Air Supply loss, but Air Supply rolls still cost 2 rating points per ☠ until the suit is properly repaired at a facility. On failure: breach remains open; retry next turn. Improvised materials hold the immediate problem but cannot restore full integrity.

**Why it matters:** A breached suit in vacuum, hostile atmosphere, or a CBRN environment is a countdown. The Armor Sealant Kit exists precisely for this — it converts a crisis into a managed problem. Operators running sealed armor in denied environments carry at least one. *See [[Equipment]] for the Armor Sealant Kit entry.*

For contamination suits (BHZ-1, HZX-3): a breach ends contamination protection immediately. The kit restores it; improvised repair does not.
