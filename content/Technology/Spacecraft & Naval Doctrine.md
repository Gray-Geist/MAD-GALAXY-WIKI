# Spacecraft & Naval Doctrine

*The ships, weapons, and technology that define power projection across the Antevic Sector.*

---

## Naval Ship Classes

### Corvettes *(30–150 meters)*
The smallest FTL-capable naval vessels in the Sector (second only to non-FTL patrol ships). Compact powerhouses — heavily armed, small, versatile, and capable of operating with minimal crews. Roles: missile platforms, reconnaissance, customs enforcement, law enforcement.

Their high concentration of **Point Defense Cannons (PDCs)** makes them exceptional in missile defense and close-quarters battle (CQB) — though this focus sacrifices offensive capabilities like torpedoes. Still a threat to larger ships through agility, strength in numbers, and synergy with patrol vessels.

**Armaments:** Multiple PDC arrays; limited torpedo bays; specialized corvettes may employ limited lightweight drone swarms.

**Crew:** 4–6 skeleton; 12–22 full complement, including up to 6 marines. Core positions: command, operations, electronic warfare, engineer, pilot, fire control, combat ops board specialist, gunner, navigator, medic, sensor technician. VI systems automate many processes but require human oversight in combat, where slicing risks are high.

**Operational Note:** Engineered for extended independent operations. Decisions to reduce crew or over-rely on VI systems often prove fatal when systems fail or multitasking becomes untenable.

---

### Frigates *(150–300 meters)*
Multi-role ships specializing in escort, patrol, and moderate fire support. Rapid-response ships in fleet actions or independent recon missions. Known for versatility — hit-and-run tactics against larger vessels or robust anti-starfighter defenses.

**Armaments:** High-velocity autocannons and torpedo tubes; limited PDCs.

**Crew:** 20 skeleton; up to 50 full deployment. FTL-capable; suited to extended deployment with active/reserve crew rotation cycles.

**Support:** Advanced drone swarms for recon, repair, and light combat. Agile combat drones and scout drones. War bots for boarding defense. Extended deployment: small hydroponic unit for supplemental food; enhanced water-recycling system.

---

### Cruisers *(300–600 meters)*
Workhorses of the fleet. Endurance, firepower, and adaptability across many mission types. Fill the gap between defensive and offensive roles; deployed in fleet formations or on long-range solo assignments.

**Armaments:** Medium ballistic cannons, energy turrets, torpedo systems. Capable of heavy fire support and suppression. Comprehensive electronic warfare suites (jamming, countermeasures, reconnaissance).

**Crew:** 75–150 standard; up to 200 in prolonged voyages. Full medical bays, 2–3 boarding squads, auxiliary craft for away missions.

**Support:** Heavy combat drones with composite armor; tactical recon and repair drones; war bots for CQB defense; drop ships and light mechs; 2–4 starfighter or bomber bays for scalable strike support.

**Operational Capacity:** Hydroponic bays and small aeroponic chambers supplement food production; medical bay supports extended deployment.

---

### Heavy Cruisers *(600–1,200 meters)*
Multi-role heavy hitters with fortified armor and advanced weaponry. Deliver sustained high-impact assaults while maintaining defensive capabilities to resist counterattacks.

**Armaments:** Heavy plasma turrets, anti-capital ship missiles, tactical PDCs.

**Defenses:** High-grade protosteel composites and layered hulls. Fighter bays for rapid deployment of defensive craft.

**Crew:** 150–300. Redundancy-focused — each position cross-trained for combat and technical duties.

**Support:** Advanced drones for heavy combat, electronic warfare, and tactical recon; war bots positioned for anti-boarding defense; full arsenal of mechs, armored transports, drop ships; small wing of starfighters and bombers — Heavy Cruisers serve as staging platforms for both aerial and ground assaults.

**Operational Capacity:** Extensive hydroponic and aquaponic systems; full medical ward; cryogenic storage.

---

### Destroyers *(1,000–2,000 meters)*
Fleet enforcers tailored for offensive operations against both large ships and small squadrons. Weaponry includes spinal-mounted railguns, energy lances, and precision torpedo launchers — giving them dominance in large-scale conflicts.

**Defenses:** Layered composite armor and adaptive electronic warfare suites capable of disrupting communications and misleading enemy targeting.

**Crew:** 200–500. Specialized teams for electronic warfare, close-range defense, and strike coordination. Often serve as flagship escorts or fleet anchors in contested regions.

**Support:** High-capacity drones built for heavy assault and tactical jamming; drone swarm defense; war bots for ground and boarding combat. **Unique among non-Battleship classes:** armored vehicle bays for deploying ground assault units (tanks, mechs, heavy armored transports); limited fighter and bomber capacity.

**Operational Capacity:** Hydroponic farms, protein synthesis units, two specialized medical bays.

---

### Battleships *(2,000–5,000 meters)*
The powerhouses of the Sector's naval forces — designed exclusively for destroying enemy ships. Multi-layered weaponry including kinetic cannons, heavy PDC arrays, and torpedo pods, supported by robust electronic warfare shielding. Double-plated armor system.

**Crew:** 500–1,200. Complete medical facilities and research labs. Equipped to act as mobile bases for smaller ships.

**Support:** High-grade precision combat drones; multi-vector attack capabilities; war bots strategically positioned for boarding defense. Hangars support multiple wings of starfighters, bombers, and interceptors. **Unlike Destroyers:** Battleships are not outfitted to deploy heavy vehicles for planetary warfare — they focus exclusively on space-superiority.

**Operational Capacity:** Robust hydroponics, bioreactors, full medical and cryogenic storage, bot repair bays; semi-autonomous for extended sieges.

---

### Carriers *(2,000–5,000 meters)*
Deployment platforms, not combat vessels. FTL-capable launch platforms for strike craft and recon ships. Hangars house hundreds of starfighters, bombers, and surveillance drones.

**Armaments:** Defensive PDCs and interceptor missiles only; operate alongside combat vessels (Destroyers, Battleships).

**Crew:** 800–1,500. Specialties span flight control, engineering, medical support, fighter operations. Continuous crew rotation cycles for strike craft operations.

**Support:** Advanced drone bays for recon, electronic warfare, and defense. War bots for internal security. Transport dropships capable of delivering ground vehicles (armored transports, light mechs) for tactical support.

**Operational Capacity:** Hydroponics and aeroponic bays; water filtration; specialized medical bays; dedicated cryogenic storage for far-away operations.

---

### Dreadnoughts *(5,000+ meters)*
The ultimate capital ships — juggernauts engineered to dominate the battlefield. Vast armament: spinal energy lances, railguns, torpedo tubes. Experimental and advanced electronic warfare suites. Near-impenetrable armor.

**Crew:** Minimum 2,000; quarters and facilities for up to 5,000 including strike teams. Act as mobile war centers orchestrating fleet-wide operations.

**Support:** Vast array of high-grade multi-role drones; heavily armored war bots and auto-mechs stationed in fortified bays to counter any boarding threat. Entire armored vehicle hangars (light recon to heavy tanks and assault mechs); multiple wings of starfighters, bombers, and interceptors.

**Operational Capacity:** Entire hydroponic farms, aquaponic systems, medical facilities, bot repair bays, advanced bioreactors. Self-sufficient mobile war zones; can lead fleets into deep space with near-endless autonomy. **Rare** — construction and upkeep costs limit them to only the wealthiest or most strategic forces.

---

### Lighthugger Hulks *(5,000+ meters)*
The colossal Savage vessels — city-sized leviathans born from Old Earth's final years, designed to carry Earth's elites as they abandoned a dying planet. They moved not by lightspeed but by crawling velocities across stretches of interstellar darkness. Over generations, the isolated dynasties aboard diverged drastically, eventually becoming the **Savages** — abhuman beings who shed their humanity to become god-like, violent zealots.

**Armaments & Defenses:** In early Savage Wars encounters, humanity discovered terrifying inexplicable technology aboard these hulks. Entire cities fell as Lighthuggers deployed weapons that absorbed the life essence of targets to feed Savage AIs or empower grotesque biomechanical beasts. The hulks themselves feature massive armored plating and occult-like energy fields that resist scans and tactical bombardment alike. Command centers were found to be disturbingly sentient — even now.

**Crew Variation:** Not every Lighthugger relied on biomechanical alterations. Some dynasties took a fully cybernetic path — minimal biological components, interiors dominated by sleek metallic designs and neuro-linked control systems that eerily blended synthetic autonomy with an occult intelligence.

**Drones & Abominations:** Some hulks carried terrible drone swarms programmed for flesh harvesting, massive war bots, and biomechanical monstrosities prowling their shadowed halls. Each hull has unique configurations. Standard features across all hulks: genetic laboratories, bio or cyber engineering chambers, and occult AI interfaces.

**Interior:** Within cavernous interiors, vast agro-fields stretch for miles, lit by artificial suns, sustaining crops modified for unknown purposes. Cities and settlements dot the internal landscape, functioning like organs in a body — each "organ" twisted from its original purpose in service of the ship's dynasty.

**Tactical Reality:** Humanity discovered that fleet battles alone often failed to destroy these hulks. The effective approach became infiltrating with small elite teams — "**Hull Reapers**" — who boarded ships to locate weak points, targeting reactors and critical systems. Known as suicide missions. Hull Reapers would overload reactors, collapse major infrastructure, or take out command centers — whatever it took to cripple the monster from within. Few ever returned. Those who survived describe vast oppressive basilicas and endless hallways ranging from biomechanical sinew to raw concrete. Every Lighthugger engaged in battle displayed different technological strengths and occult methodologies — no clear understanding of their internal workings was ever established. Engaging a Lighthugger is an act of desperation.

## Known Spacecraft

**ACLC Genevieve "The Jenny":** The Alistair Class Light Corvette — out of production for decades but popular among independent spacers for its flexibility and durability. Most remaining Alastairs have been refitted and repurposed multiple times, so their configuration and internal spaces vary. Accommodates cargo and passenger transport, exploration, and combat operations. Runs effectively understaffed — most seats in the control room should be filled at all times, especially pilot, sensors, and flight engineer.

## Spacecraft Weapons

**Burst Turret:** Chemical arm turret mounted on ships or large vehicles; extremely high rate of fire (similar to a gatling gun). On a ship, intended for anti-personnel fire suppression from low altitudes.

**Prometheus Shotgun *(Capital Class):*** Similar in design to a Pulse Howitzer. A low-yield nuke turns a foam filler into plasma which propels a spread of tungsten pellets at a target — less fast or concentrated than a Casaba Howitzer but useful in close-range ship battles.

**Pulse Howitzer *(Capital Class):*** Also known as a "Pulse unit." Focuses all diffuse concussive energy of a nuclear explosion down into one spot. The explosive charge vaporizes the ablative tungsten disc at the top, propelling resulting super-heated plasma toward the target. Highly effective against hardened targets; not useful against spread-out soft targets like cities. **Single-use — discarded into space after firing.**

## Spacecraft Propulsion & Navigation

**Chained Eidolon Reaction Drive (Skip-Drive):** Achieves FTL speeds by harnessing the turbulent forces "stitched into" the galaxy — the [[Cosmic Strands]]. A ship equipped with a Chained Eidolon Reaction Drive is known as an interstellar space vessel. See [[Skip Drives & FTL Travel]].

**Lot Drive *(Sub-FTL):*** Also known as sub-FTL or "Lot engines." Creates a reaction that breaks down Lothranium-289 pellets into charged particles, which are expelled to create thrust — powering sub-FTL flight during interstellar cruising. Lot Drives optimize engine efficiency by taking advantage of Lothranium-289's quantum decay, which emits low-intensity exotic particles enabling precise navigation and extended operation in harsh environments. *Mishandling Lothranium-289 or a Lot Drive can destabilize critical subsystems or cause localized quantum disruptions.*

**The Eidolon Computer ("e-comp"):** The ship's navigational brain. Strand navigation data from the Strandlock Encoder is fed into the e-comp, which interprets it to guide the skip drive through Strand turbulence, cycles, and obstacle ratios — ensuring optimal efficiency and stability.

**Strandlock Encoder:** Highly advanced cipher systems designed to encrypt and decrypt data critical to interstellar operations — especially vital for maintaining the secrecy and security of Cosmic Strand navigation data. Each star nation possesses its unique version; all share the same colloquial designation, reflecting its universal importance.

**Repulsor Engine:** Also known as a "repulsor." High-maintenance and often fragile technology, paired with anti-gravity technology, capable of levitating an object. Included on many vehicle types. **Only works within a gravity well** — the technology requires mass to push against.

**Acceleration Dampeners:** Manipulate gravity to compensate for the intense accelerations that starships undergo during high-speed maneuvers, preventing occupants from being killed by excessive g-forces.

## Spacecraft Equipment

**Auto-Doc:** Automatic medical device capable of diagnosing and healing an individual from injury or illness. Quality is based on hardware and software. Can perform limited structural treatments and basic surgery in addition to providing medication. Only the most advanced auto-docs are designed to replace an actual medical crew member — and these are rare.

**Bulkhead Bags ("BBs"):** Inflatable barriers that automatically inflate and seal off a corridor in the event of a breach or fire. Thick enough under pressure that a person in a space suit or armor can slide between the bags without leaving a gap, enabling repair crews to access emergencies without cracking open an airlock. Inflated by a spring-loaded rod that smashes an ampoule of sulfamic acid onto a pellet of sodium nitrite, producing nitrogen gas. Single-use; easily replaced. Replacing spent barriers is standard in provisioning procedures.

**La-Z-Boy *(Advanced Discovery Scanner):*** Highly expensive. Identifies signal sources and astronomical objects (planets, moons, asteroid clusters) within a star system. Generates exploration data that can be sold to cartographics corporations or used for mission data.

**N-Hull *(Nano-Hull):*** Nano-technology for emergency hull sealing in combat or rescue operations involving a "major hull breach." N-Hull modules come as hexagonal prisms in various sizes — all carriable by hand or magnetically attachable to suits. Placed in groups of 3–4 around the damaged area, they network with one another to project an energy field across the damaged hull. This field seals the interior from space. Nanobots then "crawl" along the energy field like a spider web and begin healing the breach with **nano-steel** — sealing it permanently until proper repairs can occur. *Nanosteel is not as strong as proto-steel (standard spacecraft hull material) but will withstand moderate damage.*
