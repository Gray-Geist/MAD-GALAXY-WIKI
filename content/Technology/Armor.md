# Armor

> [!info] Availability Codes

*Six centuries of warfare have produced a protective equipment ecosystem ranging from laminate law enforcement vests to fully sealed military battle systems and powered exoskeleton chassis. Below is the current state of the art.*

---

## Personal Armor

Personal armor in the Antevic Sector runs from the cheapest laminate vest a law enforcement budget can afford to purpose-built vacuum-rated contractor rigs — there is armor you buy and armor you are issued — the difference is not just money, but what someone has decided your life is worth.

---

### CRV-4 Composite Vest

Passes for body armor when the organization issuing it doesn't expect a real fight. Lightweight laminate construction over a rigid ceramic insert, no environmental sealing, no integrated monitoring — just enough protection to stop a handgun round and satisfy an insurance liability clause. Standard issue for planetary law enforcement, private security details, and anyone whose employer calls their job "low-risk." On a battlefield it is nearly invisible, which is appropriate, because the people wearing it generally are too.

---

### SCA-12 Standard Combat Armor

Garrison and militia armor — the gear issued to soldiers who are expected to hold a line rather than break one. This is what you hand a soldier when you need them equipped fast and you're not sure how bad it will get. It has the vitals monitoring, which means the medic gets your biometrics the moment something goes wrong — that one feature has saved more lives than the armor plates themselves. It shows up in surplus, on militia rosters, in the armories of colonial governments that can't afford better and know it.

---

### MCA-7 Medium Combat Armor

Combat armor without the full military footprint. The MCA-7 is where the private sector intersects with real violence — medium-grade composites durable enough for sustained combat, light enough to function in it, and clean enough to be purchased without explaining too much to anyone. Plus vitals monitoring, optional TacLink integration, but no vacuum seal. Half the mercenary companies in the Sector run MCA-7 as their standard kit. The other half wish they did.

---

### MCA-6V "Vac-Rated"

There are places in the Sector where the air itself is the threat, and for those places the MCA-6V exists. Preferred by salvage crews, void contractors, and mercenaries who operate across multiple environments without wanting to swap kits between them. The vacuum rating adds cost and a little weight but removes the need to think about what the atmosphere is doing to you when other things are also trying to.

---

## Battle Armor

Battle armor emerged from the Savage Wars as the minimum viable kit for a soldier expected to survive sustained contact — not just stop a round, but operate sealed against hostile atmospheres, maintain a TacLink feed, and still be trackable by a medic when things went wrong. Each star nation has or outsources its own BCL standard fitted with a closed architecture keyed to their own encrypted network. The BCL line represents that doctrine codified: a hierarchy of protection, weight, and capability built around the assumption that the environment itself is as dangerous as the enemy. These are not suits you wear to feel safer. They are systems built around the premise that you are going somewhere the galaxy is actively trying to kill you.

---

### BCL-3 Battle Armor

The front-line military dress and the baseline against which every other armor in this tier is measured. Full environmental seal, integrated TacLink, vitals monitoring, air supply — everything a combat soldier needs to function in a denied environment and be tracked and treated when they go down. It is heavier than anything below it, and you feel it over the course of a long patrol. Surplus flows out of the military pipeline and into contractor hands at a steady rate. A used BCL-3 bought from a cagey dealer is still BCL-3.

---

### BCL-3D "Drop Variant"

The armor of the drop insertion specialist, also known as "Helljumpers" — this is an advanced BCL-3 chassis with extended air supply and pod interface hardpoints built directly into the frame. It does not announce what it is on the outside. What it carries is logistics: the extended air supply for long operational holds in denied environments, and a telemetry handshake with the drop pod that feeds the wearer a pre-landing picture of the insertion zone before they hit the ground. Who wears it is a narrower population than who wears BCL-3. These are units that arrive without a ship beneath them, in places that aren't expecting them, and need the armor to support that specific kind of work. Outside those units, a BCL-3D in the wild means asking pointed questions about how it got there.

---

## Assisted Combat Personnel Armor (ACPA)

ACPA, or power armor — evolved out of the Savage Wars as the only reliable response to opponents in heavy carapace that standard arms couldn't break — a powered exoskeleton built around the principle that if you can't outrun the scale of violence the Savages brought, you absorb it instead. The first time you step into an ACPA chassis and feel the servos come online, you understand immediately why infantry without them are a different category of soldier. The chassis — the linear frame at the heart of every ACPA unit — does not just carry the suit's weight — it multiplies everything you do, and it does it quietly, translating servo power into an extension of battlefield power. Variants define their role by what the chassis prioritizes: protection, speed, stealth, firepower. Operating ACPA is not simply wearing better armor. It is becoming a different kind of problem on a battlefield — one that requires a different kind of solution to remove. Each star nation has or outsources its own ACPA standard fitted with a closed architecture keyed to their own encrypted network.

---

### ACPA-R "Republic Standard"

The jack of all trades — this is the chassis everything else is compared against — not because it is the best at any one thing, but because it is adequate at all of them. General purpose, general issue, unremarkable yet it makes the user remarkable in the best possible ways. The soldiers who wear it don't want a specialty chassis. They want armor that works, and the R delivers that without ceremony. It is the most common ACPA in the Sector by volume, which means it is also the most common powered armor a hostile force will ever have to solve for.

---

### ACPA-91 "Jackrabbit"

Speed before armor, always. The Jackrabbit is the ACPA for operators who trust their ability to not be where the fire is more than they trust their plates to stop it. The lateral thrust system — low-profile vectoring jets recessed into the hip and shoulder chassis — can create rapid sidestep movement with a control signature so clean that the motion reads almost like instinct from inside the suit. Recon units love it. So do operators who need to close a distance faster than an opponent can track. The chassis is not built to take sustained hits — it is built to not receive one.

---

### ACPA-B "Bulwark"

The Bulwark exists to be shot at. The integrated shield system is not a hand-held accessory — it is wired directly into the chassis' front arc — making the Bulwark's forward profile the hardest thing on a standard breach team to neutralize. Designed for the point-man role: first through the door, first into the corridor, the chassis that absorbs the initial response so the rest of the team can work. The additional frontal protection comes with the same mobility profile as the R standard — no sprint restriction, no speed penalty — which means the Bulwark can push and push fast. Operators who run it describe the experience of stacking at a breach point in full Bulwark as one of the few moments in combat where you are quietly certain you are the most dangerous thing in the immediate vicinity.

---

### ACPA-86 "Swinery"

This is a siege engine that has agreed to carry a soldier inside it. Immune to infantry explosives, frag, and small arms — it is incapable of sprinting. It does not pursue or flank. It stands in a place and makes that place extremely expensive to contest. Operators know this going in. The job of the Swinery is not to maneuver — it is to be immovable. In the right place at the right time, nothing in a standard infantry engagement can displace it. Finding one outside a siege detachment or MRRII black market channel means someone paid an extraordinary amount for a very specific kind of problem.
