# Transportation

*"The same general idea applies to automobiles — externally. Most are still basically a box on wheels."*

---

## Overview

Transportation technology has yielded steady improvements but nothing aggressively staggering. A century of economic strife and civil unrest before leaving Old Earth, followed by six centuries of the Decline period and the Savage Wars, **snuffed out aggressive new technological research**. The results are vehicles and systems that feel evolutionary rather than revolutionary — a testament to robust, time-tested engineering that continues to evolve without sacrificing its fundamental principles.

---

## Ground Transportation

Battery-powered vehicles dominate ground travel. The size of typical vehicles makes batteries highly efficient, and the infrastructure for rapid charging is well-developed. Recent advances in **graphene composite and quantum dot array battery chemistry** have pushed energy storage to new limits. **Autonomous navigation systems** and **VI-assisted route optimization** streamline urban mobility, reducing human error and increasing efficiency.

Most vehicles have **[[NeuroSyn & Cybernetics#HardLight Window Technology|HardLight Window]]** tech installed to protect occupants from external hazards.

Despite futuristic upgrades, the basic design endures: a box on wheels. Auto manufacturers have developed imaginative designs, but the fundamental principle of ground travel from A to B remains effectively unchanged from Old Earth.

## Public Transportation (Maglev Levs)

**Superconductor magnets** have made it possible to build extremely cheap and durable **magnetic levitation (maglev) rail services**. Riding on magnetic cushions, these maglev trains — called **"levs"** — are one of the major transportation resources in major cities, colonies, and stations, often financed by corporations or governments.

![[tech_transportation_public.png]]

### Lev Infrastructure
- **Inside city limits:** Usually underground
- **Suburbs and outer areas:** Run on high pillars
- **Space stations:** Often part of the station's structural framework
- **Restricted lines:** Routes to corporate or military areas are sealed off, requiring entry codes or passes

**Corporate lev stations** are always clean, well-lit, and well-guarded. **City lev stations** are typically lower standard, though most cities run police patrols to control crime and vandalism.

**Ticketing:** Charged at rates per station passed. Purchased from automatic machines, store outlets, or via the Extranet.

## Mass Transit VI (LIMPTVI)

In super-metroplexes where traffic volume exceeds any human or standard VI capacity to manage, municipalities deploy **LIMPTVI** — Low-Intelligence Mass Public Transport Virtual Intelligence. These are city-scale VI systems purpose-built to manage automated transport fleets: sled cabbies, grav-trains, shuttle loops, and orbital connectors.

LIMPTVI operates within strict parameters — routing, scheduling, collision avoidance, and load balancing — without the need for higher cognitive function. Their intelligence is deliberately kept low: a LIMPTVI does not reason, plan, or deviate. It optimizes. All sled cabbies, grav-trains, and automated transit vehicles within its network are subordinate nodes, feeding and receiving data in real time.

Cities like **Nagrindr on Helgoth** — vast, overcast metroplexes with constant traffic across hundreds of districts — rely entirely on LIMPTVI to keep arterial transit flowing.

## Hover Transportation

Hover technology relies on **anti-gravitic generators** and **repulsor fields** to suspend vehicles above the ground. The system works by bending space-time around the vehicle — the anti-gravitic generators warp the vehicle's world-lines, pulling space underneath it, while impeller units channel curved world-lines through the vehicle's core to keep it suspended. Grav-plates provide stability and control, acting as brake and propulsion assists.

Hover technology is a **strategic resource** rather than a universal standard. The constant power demands and vulnerability of anti-gravitic systems make it impractical for general adoption. For the average colony or military force, **wheels and treads remain the backbone of transportation** due to reliability, low cost, and ease of repair.

> Hover tech is reserved for situations where **superior mobility and adaptability are worth the investment** — a luxury or tactical niche.

### Military Applications

Hover vehicles in military use are **specialized tools** — powerful but niche, deployed when traditional wheeled or treaded vehicles are at a disadvantage.

**Advantages:**
- Multi-terrain capability (swamps, deserts, water, soft sand, snow, fractured landscape) with minimal speed loss
- Immunity to pressure-activated mines and most ground-based traps
- High speed and maneuverability over rough terrain without roads
- Elevated firing platforms — adjustable height for improved angles and sensor reconnaissance
- **Logistics Crates:** Deliver ammunition and supplies across hazardous terrain; NeuroSyn-connected systems track designated personnel or vehicles for rapid resupply under fire

**Disadvantages:**
- Energy-intensive: maintaining the hover field drastically reduces operational range
- Highly vulnerable to EMPs — anti-gravitic systems and impellers can be shut down entirely
- Complex field maintenance requires specialized knowledge and tools
- Recoil from heavy weapons destabilizes hover vehicles without advanced compensation systems
- Strong winds and atmospheric disturbances severely affect stability

### Civilian Applications

Hover tech is rarer in civilian use, primarily due to complexity and cost. Deployed mainly for industrial operations or specialized transport where traditional vehicles cannot operate effectively.

**Advantages:**
- All-terrain capability: ideal for search-and-rescue in disaster zones, crossing debris fields and flooded areas
- No roads or tracks required: ideal for developing colonies and off-grid locations
- Low physical impact: does not erode terrain or damage infrastructure — environmentally preferable in sensitive areas
- **Hover-crates:** widely used for cargo transport in warehouses, construction, and remote outposts; autonomously follow users via NeuroSyn and HUD interfaces; anti-gravitic lift handles heavy loads over rough terrain; require frequent maintenance

**Disadvantages:**
- Prohibitively expensive to build and maintain
- High energy demand even for civilian models — frequent recharging or fuel stops required
- **Floaty** handling — advanced training required to operate safely, especially in urban or confined environments
