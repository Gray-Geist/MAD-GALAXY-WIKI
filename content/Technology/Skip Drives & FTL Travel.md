# Skip Drives & FTL Travel

> *"Think of a starship using FTL as a stone skipping across a vast star-spangled basin."*

![[Star_Travel_Cosmic_Stands_Passages.jpg]]


---

## The Chained Eidolon Reaction Drive

Interstellar travel is made possible by the **Chained Eidolon Reaction Drive** — universally called the **skip-drive**. It achieves faster-than-light speeds by harnessing turbulent forces "stitched into" the galaxy called **[[Cosmic Strands]]**.

Skip-drives are expensive, bulky, and only ships at **corvette class and larger** possess the cargo capacity to carry one. For every vessel capable of FTL travel, there are approximately **five that cannot** make interstellar jumps. These sub-stellar vessels are confined to their current system.

A ship equipped with a skip-drive is known as an **interstellar space vessel**.

---

## How FTL Works

FTL travel is not a single jump or a sustained burst of speed — it is a series of **instantaneous jumps (skips)** separated by prolonged periods of recharging and recalculation.

### Jumps
- Jumps can be made in any direction, but are highly inefficient without a [[Cosmic Strands|Strand]]
- Strands act as **efficiency multipliers**, increasing range per jump
- Jumps have a limited range without Strands
- Each jump requires calculation time and a recharge period

### FTL Limitations
Jumps are **impossible inside gravity fields** (planetary orbits). Gravitonic Interference (GI) causes catastrophic results. Any attempted jump outside of parameters will:
- Void the drive system warranty
- Carry a severe prison sentence
- Likely destroy the vessel

> ***See: [[Cosmic Strands#A Catastrophe|A Catastrophe]]** — a famous example of a gravity-well jump failure, with billions of views across the Sector.*

### Slowboating
The majority of transit time is spent **slowboating** — traveling at sub-FTL speeds through a system while the skip drive recharges or while navigating to/from FTL Launch Points.

## Navigation Infrastructure

### FTL Launch Points (Beacons)
Navigator Survey vessels place **FTL Launch Point beacons** within each system, marking the safest point of FTL ingress and egress from Strands. All FTL jumps begin and end at or near these beacons.

### Passages
A plotted course of combined jumps along Strands and slowboating legs is called a **passage**. Navigational data for passages is a valuable resource, often jealously held by those in power. Each segment ends at a waypoint called an **anchorage**.

### Strand Quality & Travel Time

![[Star_Travel_FTL_Diagram.jpg]]

| Strand Type | Base Travel Time | Modifiers |
|---|---|---|
| Super Strand | 1 week (d3) | −1d3 weeks with advanced skip drive or needling |
| Stable Strand | 1.5 weeks (d4) | −1d2 weeks with advanced skip drive or needling |
| Weak Strand | 2 weeks (d6) | −1 week with advanced skip drive or needling |

### Transition Delays

| Transition | Delay Added |
|---|---|
| Super → Stable | 1–4 days (d4) |
| Stable → Weak | 2–6 days (d6) |
| Weak → Super | 3–8 days (d8) |

## Strand Cracking & Needling

### Cracking
Trailblazing new Strands beyond settled regions is called **cracking** — an incredibly arduous venture performed by trained **[[Polymath & Poly-psykers|Navigators]]**. Because Strands change over time, Navigators must also maintain and update navigational data to keep travel efficient and safe.

### Needling
Over the years, different nations have refined their skip drives through a method called **needling**, increasing travel efficiency versus less optimized vessels.
