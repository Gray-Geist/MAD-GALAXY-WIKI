# Availability Codes

Every weapon, pharmaceutical, piece of gear, and augmentation in the Sector carries an **Availability Code (AV)** — a shorthand for how easy something is to acquire and under what conditions.

---

## The Six Codes

| Code | Label | Typical Context |
|---|---|---|
| **C** | Common | Startup colonies, small stations, villages |
| **R** | Rare | Established colonies, major stations, cities |
| **RR** | Very Rare | Major trading hubs, large cities |
| **I** | Illegal | Licensed items traded without a license |
| **II** | Very Illegal | Black market only |
| **M** | Military | Military issue — not for civilian sale |

**C — Common:** You can find this at the local trading post. No questions asked, no paperwork, no wait.

**R — Rare:** Requires a real supply chain. Available in established settlements and cities, but not everywhere — and not always in stock.

**RR — Very Rare:** You need to be in a major hub or a large city, and you may still have to ask around. This is the threshold where having the right contacts starts to matter.

**I — Illegal:** This item has legitimate licensed uses, but you don't have a license. Trading it without one is a criminal act in most jurisdictions — seller and buyer both carry risk.

**II — Very Illegal:** There is no legitimate civilian channel for this item. If you're buying it, you're buying it from a criminal, in a place where criminals operate, at a price that reflects the risk. The transaction itself is evidence of intent.

**M — Military:** Issued through military procurement and not available through civilian channels by any legal means. Finding one in the wild means someone lost it, sold it, or died with it.

---

## Combined Codes

Items often carry multiple codes concatenated together — this means the item spans more than one availability context simultaneously.

**IC** — Illegal and Common: This item is technically controlled, but it's everywhere anyway. You can find it easily; you just can't buy it legally without the right paperwork. Common on frontier worlds where enforcement is thin.

**ICM** — Illegal, Common, and Military: Widely available on the grey market, legally restricted for civilian use, and also standard military issue — meaning it flows from military surplus into civilian and criminal channels constantly.

**IIC** — Very Illegal and Common: Banned outright, but so widely produced or distributed that availability isn't the problem — only the legal exposure is.

**IIR** — Very Illegal and Rare: Black market only, and not easy to find even there. You need a serious contact or a serious amount of patience.

**IIM** — Very Illegal and Military: The military uses it; civilians are not supposed to have it at all. What's on the black market came out of a military supply chain — stolen, diverted, or sold by someone who shouldn't have.

**MC** — Military and Common: Standard military issue that has flooded the civilian market through surplus, decommissioning, or simple ubiquity. Legal to own in most jurisdictions, but originally designed and issued for military use.

**MRR** — Military and Very Rare: Military issue that hasn't leaked into civilian channels in any meaningful quantity. Finding one outside a military context requires exceptional access or exceptional luck.

**MRRII** — Military, Very Rare, and Very Illegal: The most restricted category. Military issue only, almost never seen outside official channels, and completely banned from civilian possession. If you have one, someone will want to know how.

## Cyberware & Implant Codes

Cyberware and bioware can exist in two distinct tiers simultaneously — a legal specialist-clinic version and an illegal black-market version, or a civilian grade and a military-spec grade of the same underlying technology. These dual-context codes encode both tiers in a single tag.

**RRII** — Very Rare (licensed specialist channel) / Very Illegal (black market): This augmentation can be obtained legally, but only through a licensed specialist clinic — the kind that requires appointments, documentation, and significant expense. The same procedure exists on the black market, but obtaining it there is a serious criminal act. Who performs it and under what conditions makes all the difference.

**RM** — Rare (civilian/industrial tier) / Military (military-spec tier): Two grades of the same technology exist. The civilian version moves through specialist commercial channels — uncommon but accessible. The military-spec version is a procurement item: higher performance, closed supply chain, not for sale.

**RMRR** — Rare (civilian tier) / Military + Very Rare (military-spec tier): The civilian version is available through specialist suppliers — not mass-market, but findable. The military-spec version rarely leaves military channels and is significantly harder to obtain even through exceptional access.

*Note: MRRII (Military, Very Rare, and Very Illegal) also applies to cyberware and bioware — see the Combined Codes section above.*

---

## Acquiring Outside Normal Context

If you're trying to get something outside its listed availability context — buying military hardware as a civilian, sourcing a Very Illegal item in a city that has functioning law enforcement — it takes time and effort.

**Mechanics:** One Stretch of searching. Roll **OBSERVATION** in abandoned or remote locations; roll **MANIPULATION** in populated ones. The GM sets the difficulty: −1 to −3 dice depending on how far outside normal context you're operating. No roll is required if you have an established contact who deals in that item.
