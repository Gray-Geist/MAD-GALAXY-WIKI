# Communications

> *"Communication across an entire galaxy was clearly no simple matter."*

![[communications_art1.png]]

## The Problem

Conventional signals traveling through the vacuum of space take years to reach from one solar system to another. For the first century after leaving Earth (the **Exploration** era), space pioneers had no solution — supply lines were thin and communication even thinner.

---

## C-Boats: Communication of the Past

![[communications_cboat1.png]]
![[communications_cboat2.png]]

In **100 AE**, an ancient profession was reborn: the **courier**.

Couriers ran ships called **C-Boats** — their unending mission to connect the ever-expanding human corner of the galaxy via communication and data. They were the first to begin mastering the [[Cosmic Strands]], running along stellar winds and fiery energies like sea vessels on the edge of storms, trailblazing valuable navigational data.

C-Boats ranged from swift and light vessels to heavily defended ones carrying armed turrets, immense data stores, and high-capacity transceivers. They carried news, banking information, email, and other data along **the mains** (most-traveled routes). Away from the mains, free traders carried mail and data to outlying worlds. Governments and militaries developed their own parallel networks. **Signal Runners** exploited encrypted transmissions, trading in data deemed illegal.

### The C-Boat Process (Historical)
1. Sender composes a message
2. Once per data cycle, outgoing data is transmitted to a C-boat in orbit
3. The C-boat travels to the FTL Launch Point
4. The C-boat travels to the destination system via a long series of individual skips
5. The C-boat slowboats to the destination planet's orbit
6. Data is uploaded to the local network; outgoing data is downloaded; return trip begins

Later improvements included externally transferable storage drives and **C-Boat Circuits** — teams running coordinated loops. But fuel costs were high and loss rates brutal.

## The Extranet: Communication of Today

![[communications_art2.png]]

In **1153 AE** — sixty-two years before the Colonization era — a breakthrough arrived: the **Extranet**.

The Extranet is a vast architecture formed by specialized **Extranet-comm buoys (e-comm buoys)** placed throughout the Sector. It is not a Sector-wide digital communication landscape, but a stellar (within-system) solution to an interstellar problem.

### How It Works
1. **Extranet towers** are built in cities or colonies
2. **Buoys** (or **superbuoys** for bandwidth nexuses) are manufactured and deployed
3. Buoys are aligned into an **Extranet-array**, stretching from population centers to FTL Launch Points
4. Buoys are telemetrically aligned with partner buoys light-minutes away (each light-minute = 18 million km) via **tight beam laser corridor** — the space-age equivalent of fiber-optic cable
5. Data is encoded, sent to the nearest tower, relayed to an e-comm buoy, and transmitted through the array
6. C-boats can now gather data at the *edge* of systems and quickly return to FTL Launch Points — no more long slowboats through the entire system

![[communications_art3.png]]

### Priority System
In heavily populated systems, billions of people overload finite bandwidth. Data is sent in carefully timed bursts, prioritized:
1. Organizations responsible for national or system security
2. Megacorps and media conglomerates
3. Remaining space sold to highest bidders → Extranet provider corporations → resold to individual subscribers

Depending on the provider and package, individuals can get interstellar messages out in multi-week, monthly, or even yearly bursts.

### Extranet Redoubts
Located 3–10 light-minutes from FTL Launch Points, **Extranet Redoubts** are fortified control nexuses that transform data from arriving C-boats to the Extranet. They cost enormous amounts — measured in gigacredits (BCr) — making them only feasible for major powers.

![[communications_art4.png]]

### Limitations
- Not every system has the Extranet — only heavily populated ones
- The Savage Wars destroyed large portions of the Extranet; haphazard wartime rebuilding created persistent blackouts
- Blackouts range from minutes (affecting some buoys) to days (affecting an entire system)
- During blackouts, C-boats step up to carry data packets

## Quantum Entanglement Communicators (QECs): Instant & Unbreakable

![[communications_qec1.png]]

**QECs** exploit quantum entanglement: a change to one particle of an entangled pair affects the other instantaneously, anywhere in the universe.

![[communications_qec2.png]]

### Advantages
- **Instantaneous** communication over any distance — no C-boats, no Extranet dependency
- **Cannot be intercepted** — no wiretapping possible

### Limitations
- Extremely expensive and difficult to produce
- Severely limited bandwidth — a single entangled particle transmits only one qubit (quantum bit) at a time
- Exclusively **point-to-point** — cannot peer-to-peer network or feed the Extranet

![[communications_qec3.png]]

### Military Application
The most practical military use is at headquarters level: each colony and fleet flagship maintains a QEC. All pairs connect to a central hub station. If attacked, the facility signals the hub to transmit to every other fleet and colony simultaneously.

**Critical vulnerability**: Destruction of the central hub collapses the entire network.

## Quantum Stasis Zone (QSZ)

A **Quantum Stasis Zone** is a secure enclosure — most commonly a hardened safe room — engineered to guarantee absolute electromagnetic containment. No electromagnetic particle can enter or escape the zone. A QSZ is the only environment where the absolute containment of classified information can be guaranteed, whether that information exists on a data link, quantum chip, or encoded by proteins into a biological sequence.

Entry requires bio-signature recognition by the containment field. Unrecognized biomaterial — from a stray cell to an entire person — is dissolved on contact with the field boundary, separated down to isolated amino acids. The deterrent is absolute.

QSZs are used by governments, megacorps, and intelligence agencies for meetings, data storage, and interrogations where no signal leakage of any kind can be tolerated.
