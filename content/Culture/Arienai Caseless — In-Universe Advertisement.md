# Arienai Caseless — In-Universe Advertisement

*An in-world commercial document distributed by Arienai Corporation. Reproduced here as a primary source artifact of Sector commercial culture.*

---

External propellant caseless rounds are manufactured where the body of the round itself is the propellant that burns up. Electrothermal-chemical (ETC) uses electricity to ignite a chemical reaction at the base of the round causing it to ignite and fly down the barrel, spinning due to the rifling, exiting the weapon essentially as a solid fuel rocket, burning up its heavily compressed powdered outer layer as it travels toward its target. There is no ejected physical casing, but there is build up over time of spent propellant powder, requiring regular maintenance to clean away.

Caseless rounds coupled with ETC innovations have led to high muzzle velocities with less energy input compared to other electric propulsion systems like railguns, leading to more compact and powerful weapons — but they require a power source like a battery, and managing the heat generated from the electrical reaction was, until modern times, a complex problem without resolution.

## The Difference Between Arienai and Other Caseless Ammunition

The single biggest difference between caseless rounds made by Arienai and all other brands is the compound. **Arienai does not use wax; all other brands do.**

Caseless rounds are usually given a final coat of clear wax following their final compression, in order to make the round waterproof.

---

## The Neus Kapitel Group (NKG) Argument FOR Waterproofing Rounds

Waterproofing is important if you are planning to store your caseless rounds over long periods, in damp places like basements and bunkers, or in hunting cabins. Especially for hunters who have to be ready for bad weather, knowing your rounds are safe from water is more than a source of pride — it's just good sense. Arienai rounds are **also** known for producing a lot more muzzle flash and smoke, even colored smoke, depending on the color of the rounds being fired. Any good soldier knows that muzzle flash and smoke can give your position away to your enemies!

---

## The Arienai Argument AGAINST Waterproofing Rounds

We are not in the business of arming anti-government would-be revolutionaries stockpiling ammunition in the ground; we are in the business of arming professionals, who depend on their weapons for their lives and livelihood instead of merely for sport or political purposes.

Arienai has worked for several years, developing an ammunition compound that is lightweight yet incredibly strong, that will hold its shape in extreme heat or cold without clumping, one of the common dangers that other brands of caseless ammunition face.

Another serious issue Arienai have managed to avoid is the residue build up in the barrels, meaning your weapons are easier to clean, and will fire further, faster, reliably.

A third advantage to Arienai ammunition is the weight. Without the wax coating, our rounds by the case are between **5-7% lighter** than other ammunition! This has a serious effect on the ability of the soldiers to carry more rounds without fatigue, and in case quantity means that more cases can be transported using less energy and/or fuel, making our rounds more cost effective.

While not waterproof, Arienai caseless ammunition **is** very water resistant, and only in danger of breaking down when fully immersed in water for a period of **hours**. We feel the benefits to our customers greatly outweigh the need for wax on our products.

Waxed caseless ammunition is waterproof, but in extreme high temperatures rounds often will clump together as the wax partially melts. Rounds in plastic cases can get stuck in their container; rounds in the magazine can melt together, causing misfires.

Every time a waxed caseless round is fired, layers of the wax are deposited inside the barrel, building up a residue over time that is hard to clean without separating the barrel and heating it to melt the wax. The alternative is to use wire brushes to slowly scour it away.

In comparison, Arienai caseless ammo has a shell of highly compressed inert ceramic powder. When fired, the powder is partially pulverized, which may (35%) vent out of the weapon in the form of colored, faintly clay-scented smoke. This has no adverse effect on round accuracy or range, but it does increase the chance of muzzle flash.
