# Arienai Caseless — The Ammunition of the Future

**Type:** In-Universe Advertisement  
**Publisher:** [[Arienai Corporation]]  
**Subject:** Caseless Ammunition vs. Waxed Competitors

---

## What Is Caseless Ammunition?

External propellant caseless rounds are manufactured so that the body of the round itself is the propellant — it burns up on firing. **Electrothermal-chemical (ETC)** technology uses electricity to ignite a chemical reaction at the base of the round, causing it to ignite and fly down the barrel. The round exits the weapon spinning from the rifling, burning up its heavily compressed powdered outer layer as it travels toward its target.

There is no ejected physical casing — but residue from spent propellant powder builds up over time, requiring regular cleaning.

Caseless rounds coupled with ETC innovations deliver **high muzzle velocities with less energy input** than other electric propulsion systems like railguns — resulting in more compact, more powerful weapons. They do require a power source (battery), and managing heat generated by the electrical reaction was, until recently, an unresolved engineering problem.

## The Arienai Difference

### The Key Issue: Wax

Caseless rounds are typically given a final coat of clear wax after final compression to make the round waterproof. **Arienai does not use wax. Every other brand does.**

---

### The NKG Argument *For* Waterproofing

*(Neus Kapitel Group, primary competing manufacturer)*

> Waterproofing is important for long-term storage in damp environments — basements, bunkers, hunting cabins. For soldiers preparing for bad weather, knowing rounds are water-safe is just good sense. Arienai rounds are also known for producing significantly more muzzle flash and smoke (including colored smoke depending on round color). Any good soldier knows muzzle flash and smoke reveal your position.

---

### The Arienai Argument *Against* Waterproofing

> We are not in the business of arming anti-government revolutionaries stockpiling ammunition in the ground. We arm **professionals** — those who depend on their weapons for their lives and livelihood, not sport or politics.

Arienai has developed an ammunition compound that is **lightweight yet incredibly strong** — holding shape in extreme heat or cold without clumping, a common failure point for competitors.

**Three core advantages over waxed caseless:**

| Advantage | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **No barrel buildup** | Wax deposits build up inside barrels over time, requiring barrel disassembly and heat treatment or wire-scouring to remove. Arienai rounds produce no wax residue — easier cleaning, greater range, better reliability. |
| **Weight reduction** | Without the wax coating, Arienai rounds are **5–7% lighter per case**. Soldiers carry more rounds without fatigue; logistics require less energy and fuel per transport. |
| **Heat resistance** | Waxed rounds in extreme heat can partially melt and clump together — rounds in magazines fuse, causing misfires. Arienai rounds are not susceptible to this failure. |

---

### The Tradeoff

Arienai rounds are **not waterproof** — only water-resistant. They will break down when fully submerged in water for several hours. Arienai's position: the operational benefits outweigh this limitation for professional users.

---

## Technical Notes

Arienai caseless ammunition uses a shell of **highly compressed inert ceramic powder** rather than wax. When fired, the powder is partially pulverized — approximately **35% vents from the weapon as colored, faintly clay-scented smoke**. This does not adversely affect accuracy or range but does increase muzzle flash probability.
