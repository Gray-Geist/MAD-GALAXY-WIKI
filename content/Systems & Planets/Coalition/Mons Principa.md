# Mons Principa

**Planet:** [[Alatyr System#Sot Prime|Sot Prime]]  
**System:** [[Alatyr System]] · Core, Rose Sea  
**Nation:** Coalition  
**Status:** Destroyed — fell to the Savages, 1324 AE; site glassed with the rest of Sot Prime during the Requite Dilemma  
**Population at height:** ~100 million metro — the largest city in known space of its era

![[alatyr_mons_principa.png]]

---

## Overview

For three centuries, Mons Principa was what humanity pointed to when it wanted to prove the Decline was over. Capital of Sot Prime, financial and cultural heart of the Coalition, seat of the Sector's oldest continuous civilian government — a metroplex of a hundred million people built down the slopes of an artificial mountain to the shore of the Cerulan Sea, crowned with super towers and floored with the oldest human architecture in the Rose Sea. One in every twenty human beings on Sot Prime lived inside its skyline.

It was the city where the Sector kept its money, published its news, held its games, and stored its confidence. When people in the outer systems said *the Core*, they mostly meant this skyline — and the warships above it.

It stood for 62 days after the Savages came. Its fall broke the Combined Forces, emptied a world, and gave the Sector the two words — *Sot Prime* — that have meant *the worst that can happen* for two hundred years.

---

## The Founding

Mons Principa is — was — a lie about geography, and the lie is the whole city.

There is no mountain. The colony ship **CSV *Principa*** made its one-way landing on a coastal plain, and its colonists, unable to farm ground that native predators contested for decades, built *around* the grounded hull instead — ring upon ring of reinforced habitat, rubble terracing, and defensive elevation, until the ship lay at the core of a made hill. Later generations forgot the joke in the name. *Mons Principa*: the Mountain of the Principa. The founders built the mountain themselves, one bad year at a time, and buried their ship alive inside it.

The **Golden Age of Piracy** shaped the second city. When cartel raiders worked the Rose Sea, Mons Principa armored itself like a medieval fortress: street plans that funnel, districts that seal, and civic buildings constructed as strongpoints — most famously the **First Bank of Sot Prime**, a twenty-story fortress with no windows below the fifth floor and a single defended entrance behind a door that outweighed most gunships. The pirates were eventually swept from the Rose Sea; the fortresses stayed, and the boom that followed dressed them in marble.

By the century of the fall, the city wore its history in layers — founding-era brick under crystal-chandelier lobbies, cartel-era gun galleries repurposed as wine cellars, and beneath all of it, sealed and mostly forgotten, the **Old Colony**: the original settlement decks around the *Principa* herself, taken over by the government generations before for laboratories, archives, and purposes it never published. Residents called the deep levels *the house of secrets* when they thought of them at all. Mostly, they didn't.

## Beyond the Capital

Sot Prime was not one city, though history remembers it that way. Two billion people lived on the planet: a dozen secondary metroplexes in the five-to-twenty-million range strung along the temperate belts; hundreds of colony towns of tens of thousands anchoring the agricultural and extraction regions; and thousands of villages, sea-steads, and rural communes filling the country between — the ordinary settled landscape of humanity's richest world.

The record dwells on Mons Principa for a dark reason as well as a proud one: the capital is where the fall was *witnessed*. The Dynasty's drives reached the outlying towns and communes early, methodically, and far from any camera. Relief-force overflights found them the way the Vault Lines survivors feared finding everything — intact, provisioned, and empty. Nearly the entire recorded history of the fall of Sot Prime is the history of its capital and the sea-outstations offshore, because almost no one else was left to write any.

## Geography, the Corso & the Arteries

The city descends. That is the fact every visitor learned first: Mons Principa is read from top to bottom, wealth to work, Crown to water.

From the summit, seven grand radial thoroughfares run downslope like the spokes of a crushed wheel, cutting the metroplex into its great wedges. These are processional boulevards, not highways — parade routes, address-belts, the city's public face. The broadest of them — **the Corso** — was the financial artery and the address every institution wanted; sub-districts took their names from it (*Corso-North*, *Corso-Levant*). At the Corso's grand intersection above the financial district stood the **Founders' Fountain** — stone figures holding a stone ship skyward — the most photographed object on Sot Prime and the emotional center of the city.

The real traffic moved elsewhere. Like every metroplex of its scale, Mons Principa lived underground: the Vault Lines metro carried the overwhelming majority of its hundred million, and most residents of the dense districts never owned a vehicle. But a city that size still runs on wheels, and its vehicle infrastructure was continental in its own right — **three concentric ring expressways** (the Coronal at the Crown's shoulder, the Median through the mid-slopes, the Littoral along the industrial coast), elevated freight viaducts feeding the Works and the Berths, and the great **radial interchanges** where the expressway rings crossed the seven boulevards in stacked knots of ramp and tunnel. Beyond the city limits, planetary highways ran out through the agricultural belts to the secondary metroplexes and the colony towns.

In the final year, the arteries wrote their own chapter: the ring expressways became the most complete traffic jam in human history on Night Zero — a hundred-kilometer sculpture of abandoned vehicles that the occupation never bothered to clear — and the freight viaducts, high, straight, and exposed, became places the underground learned not to cross.

## The Vault Lines

Mons Principa's founders had cleared predators for decades and fought pirates for a generation; they did not believe in safe centuries. The metro system they built was a shelter network wearing a transit map.

- **The Vault Lines** — the network itself: hundreds of kilometers of deep-bore transit tunnel, rated as blast-resistant civil infrastructure from the first survey.
- **Vaults** — hardened civil-defense complexes built into the network at intervals: buried silo-habitats with blast doors, independent air handling, cisterns, stores, generator rooms, and a med bay with a basic assay lab. Designated by line and number (*Vault K-14* on Line K). Some interlink through service galleries; others were deliberately built isolated. Combined rated capacity: a fraction of the city.
- **Stations** — the ordinary transit stops: platforms, concourses, mezzanines. Deep, but not hardened, not provisioned, not sealed.

The **Civil Defense Directorate** drilled the doctrine into three generations of schoolchildren: *when the sirens hold, go down; the healthy seal in; groups stay separated.* The reverse-quarantine protocols were designed against plague and bombardment. No one who wrote them imagined the enemy they would actually be used against — or the arithmetic of a hundred million people and Vault space for a fraction of them. In the final year, roughly ninety percent of the population that survived the first night was underground. Most of them were on platforms.

## The Diadem & the Home Fleet

The richest world in human space was also its most defended — a fact that made what happened to it more terrifying, not less.

**The Diadem** was Sot Prime's orbital defense network: hundreds of hardened weapons platforms, sensor pickets, and interceptor batteries ringing the planet in tiered shells — named, with the city's usual self-regard, for the crown it guarded. The platforms themselves were automated; the network's mind lived in its **arc-stations** — twelve crewed command fortresses spaced around the planet, each directing the fire of one curved sector ("arc") of the shells, each hardened to fight on after the platforms it commanded were gone. Silo Station anchored the commercial traffic lanes; the arc-stations anchored the war the Diadem was built for. Doctrine held that no hostile fleet could survive inside the Diadem's engagement envelope, and for eighty years nothing tried.

Above and beyond it cruised the **Home Fleet** — the First Fleet of the Coalition, the navy's premier formation, whose entire purpose was the Alatyr system. In the war's fourth year the Home Fleet was the wall the Core slept behind.

Both facts were true. Neither was enough. In the weeks before Night Zero, a Savage raiding fleet allowed itself to be found savaging the shipping lanes two jumps out — a sacrifice the enemy had budgeted — and a Home Fleet task group went out to destroy it, and did. The strength that remained at Sot Prime was formidable. It was also exactly what the Dynasty had planned for.

## Institutions

**The Governorship.** Sot Prime's planetary government sat in the Crown, and in 1324 AE the seat belonged to **Governor Ilyan Vorr** — patrician, capable, and remembered by history with the particular cruelty reserved for leaders who presided over catastrophes no leader could have stopped. His great-grandson, [[Alatyr System#Solus|Aran Vorr]], governs what is left of the system today, and has never once pronounced the family's old title.

**MPPF — the Mons Principa Protective Force.** A hundred million people were policed by the Sector's largest and, by most measures, most professional metropolitan force. Its order of battle read like a small nation's: the **Patrol Bureau** and its district precinct houses; the **Tactical Division** (heavy response, siege, and counter-terror); the **Counter-Smuggling Division** working the Berths; the **Transit Police**, who knew the Vault Lines better than anyone alive except the Transit Authority's own engineers; the **Harbor Division** on the Cerulan water; the **Sky Division's** patrol sleds stacked above the ring expressways; the **Investigations Bureau** and its organized-crime and intelligence sections; and the Academy on the Littoral that trained police for half the Coalition. Post-war histories dwell on the MPPF for a dark reason: the infiltration that preceded the fall struck law enforcement and government first and hardest. The force died the way the city did — from the inside, then all at once — but its history is not only a decapitation: the stand of individual precincts in the occupied city, holding barricades for weeks without orders or relief, is among the few chapters of the fall that veterans tell willingly.

**The Civil Defense Directorate.** Keeper of the Vault Lines, the sirens, and the drills. Its volunteer wardens — armband, master keys, a hand-crank lamp — became, in the occupied city, the closest thing the underground had to a government.

**Kybern Media Group.** The Coalition's news standard, headquartered on the Corso, its casts carried Sector-wide. Kybern's field crews kept transmitting from inside the occupied metroplex for weeks after Night Zero; the surviving footage is the visual record of the fall, and the reason later generations know what the streets looked like when the drives came through them.

**Principa General.** The planetary hospital system — multiple complexes across the metroplex (Corso-North the largest), tens of thousands of beds, and the finest trauma medicine in the Core. Its physicians produced the first coherent clinical picture of what was happening in the city's final year; most did not live to see the analysis confirmed.

**The First Bank of Sot Prime.** The cartel-era fortress in Trivium Square, and the Sector's oldest continuously operating bank until 1324. Its deep vaults were never officially emptied. The legends about what remains in them — under a glassed and irradiated ruin — have killed salvage crews in every generation since.

**The Dock Unions.** The Berths ran on union labor, union boats, and union memory, with hardened storm bunkers under the freight aprons dating to the pirate century. In the occupied city, that concrete and that discipline mattered more than anyone had planned for.

**The Transit Authority.** Operator of the Vault Lines' civilian face. Its automated systems famously kept running long after the city died — trains announcing stations to no one — and, in places, are said to have kept running until the end of the world.

## The Fall (1324 AE)

*What follows is the historical outline as post-war scholarship reconstructed it. For the war's full context, see [[The Savage Wars]]; for the system-level account, see [[Alatyr System#The Hounding|The Hounding]].*

**The Quiet Year.** Military intelligence would later establish that a Savage recon force had followed a Coalition cruiser home to Alatyr, and that for 399 days a Savage Dynasty prepared the city's death from inside it. Doppelganger agents seeded themselves into government, medicine, security, and the Diadem's own watch rotations — and survivor accounts agree on the detail that still unsettles: they were *liked*. Magnetic, capable, effortlessly promotable, the kind of people rooms organized themselves around. The Citizen Registry's gene archive was compromised; a debilitating engineered virus was keyed to targeted DNA — a decapitation strike loaded through the city's own medicine. The whole operation carries the name **the Hounding** now. The people living through its final months had no name for it — only strange arrests, stranger dreams, a Registry "data incident" the Governor's office downplayed, and a general unease the markets absorbed without comment.

**Night Zero.** The reconstructed sequence, from watch logs, survivor testimony, and the Diadem's recovered arc-station recorders:

1. **The Diadem goes dark.** Infiltration sabotage drops the defense network's engagement grid — silently. For several minutes, the only human beings who know the war has begun are watch officers staring at consoles that no longer answer.
2. **Silo Station dies.** Epsilon-739-D — the largest structure humanity had ever built, one hundred thousand aboard — is destroyed *from within* in under an hour, visible from the streets of Mons Principa as a slow, silent constellation coming apart.
3. **The fleet action.** Out of the dark between the outer arcs, the Savage fleet is suddenly simply *there*, and the Home Fleet's remaining strength meets it inside a dead defense grid. The battle lasts hours, in orbit, watched from a hundred million windows as silent flashes and slow burning streaks. The Home Fleet dies fighting. It does not die cheaply — but it dies.
4. **The Diadem's last watch.** With the fleet gone, the crew of one surviving command fortress — **Arc-Station Vigil**, master of the southern polar arc — claws its sector of the grid back online and fires everything the arc has left. History remembers Vigil's watch section by name. It kills Savage ships. It cripples more. And it drives its heaviest salvos into the Dynasty's lighthugger itself, wounding the great hulk before the return fire silences the Diadem for good.
5. **The grounding.** The wounded lighthugger — a metroplex-sized juggernaut that was never built to touch a world — is forced down, and commits itself to the ground forever, beaching against the Crown, crushing the summit districts, and killing the planetary grid in cascading stripes. The Dynasty had come to stay regardless; the Diadem's dying blow chose *where*. The enemy would live in the wound humanity's defiance had made.
6. **The umbrella closes.** The surviving Savage fleet takes station overhead and begins orbital support of the ground operation. By dawn, Sot Prime is dark to the rest of the Sector, the infiltration's sleeper strikes have taken the government and the MPPF's command structure, and the survivors of the first night are going underground.

**The occupation.** What the Dynasty wanted from Mons Principa was not its destruction. Post-war analysis of the fall — survivor testimony, Kybern's recovered footage, and what the relief force found — established the pattern that gave this Dynasty its place in humanity's nightmares: herding drives sweeping the districts, populations taken alive, no bodies left anywhere, and the harvest galleries in the converted freight termini where thousands were stored, conscious, in stasis gel. The survivors in the Vault Lines had called the grounded lighthugger *the Ship* through the first weeks. After the harvest galleries were discovered, the underground settled on the name history kept: **the Granary**. The overgrown grounding site and its hull-works — the crushed Crown, the intake fields — are recorded on relief-force maps as **the Granary Fields**.

**The Relief Campaign.** Sixty-two days after Night Zero, the Combined Forces entered the Alatyr system in strength. The fleet action was fought in orbit; on the ground, Combat Controller teams inserted ahead of the advance force, and the relief campaign that followed — two weeks of corridor-fighting through a dead metroplex, with orbital kinetic strikes lighting the districts and survivor columns streaming toward the lines — briefly looked like the counterstroke the Sector had prayed for. Hundreds of thousands of civilians reached the military corridors in those two weeks. The linkups were processed at gunpoint; the Dynasty had learned to send its converted walking among the desperate, and some columns proved it.

**The Disaster at Sot Prime.** Then the Savages struck in force, and the relief campaign became the catastrophe that ended the Combined Forces as an institution. The full military history belongs elsewhere ([[The Savage Wars]]); the ground truth was simpler: the corridors died from the far end in, the withdrawal order propagated through the lines like a temperature drop, and the evacuation became triage.

**The Lift.** The only footprint in the metroplex that could cycle the evacuation was the Amphitheon — a million seats and more than a million people, very large dropships turning on burn-scarred pads under a contested sky, union boats and corporate lighters running the Cerulan shore because the stadium could not cycle fast enough. Two hundred years later, *the Lift* remains the measure against which every mass evacuation in human history is set — Dunkirk's name survives from Old Earth for the same reason. Roughly **800 million** people escaped Sot Prime in the Abandonment that followed; some **350 million** of them left aboard [[Neus Kapitel Group (NKG)]] hulls, signing colonial-labor contracts of carriage at the gangway — the founding transaction of the Helgoth colonization ([[Baadensenar System]]). More than **1.2 billion** were still on the ground when the last engines lit.

**Afterward.** The Alatian Accords formalized the Coalition's withdrawal from the system. The Savages held Alatyr for nearly a century; the Astral Alliance's return, the stalemate, and Commander Tyrus Martell's Chained Nuclear Trigger Ordinance — which ended the Savage presence and Sot Prime together — are covered under [[Alatyr System#The Requite Dilemma|the Requite Dilemma]]. Mons Principa's ruins, the Granary Fields among them, lie under that glass today.

## Today

Nothing lives at Mons Principa. The made mountain still stands — slumped, fused, and radioactive — above a shoreline the Cerulan Sea is slowly taking back. Research charters out of [[Alatyr System#Solus|Solus]] photograph it from safe orbits. Salvage syndicates whisper about the First Bank's vaults, the Undercroft's sealed decks, and the CSV *Principa* herself, still buried at the mountain's heart with whatever the government locked inside her — post-war historians hold that a deep-research program ran in those decks to the very end, known from three surviving requisition stamps only as **Project MERIDIAN**, and that the relay-lattice architecture underlying the modern [[Communications#The Extranet|Extranet]] owes an unacknowledged debt to work that died, or didn't, under the mountain. Every few years a crew goes down to find out.

Every few years, a crew doesn't come back up.
